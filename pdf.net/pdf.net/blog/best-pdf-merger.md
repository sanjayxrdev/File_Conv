## 5 Factors to Check Before Choosing a PDF Merger

Speed alone doesn’t make a good PDF merger. A tool that combines files in five seconds but scrambles your page order or drops a form field costs you more time than a slower one that gets it right. Weigh these five factors before you pick one:

1. **Setup time. **Can you start merging right away, or do you need to install software, create an account, or manage a license first? Browser-based tools remove that step entirely, which matters if you're [merging files](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-merge-pdfs) on a device you don't control.
2. **Reorder control. **Does the tool let you drag and reorder files, or even individual pages, before merging? Losing that control turns a two-minute task into a much longer one, especially with a large batch of files.
3. **Output integrity. **Does the merged PDF keep fonts, images, tables, and form fields intact? Some tools flatten or recompress pages during the merge, which can distort formatting or break fillable fields.
4. **File and page limits. **Check the actual limit before you start, especially for large document sets. It also helps to know that [Gmail caps outgoing attachments at 25MB](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/6584), so a merged file that oversteps this limit can still bounce out of your outbox. Running it through a [PDF compression tool](https://pdf.net/compress-pdf) first solves both problems.
5. **Post-merge usability. **Can you edit, sign, password-protect, or share the merged file without opening a second tool? The fewer times you switch apps, the less time you lose. If you regularly need to [create a fillable PDF](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-create-a-fillable-pdf) or [sign PDFs digitally](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-sign-pdfs-digitally) once files are combined, that continuity is worthwhile.

## Security and Privacy Considerations for Using PDF Tools

Uploading contracts, financial records, or other sensitive files to any [online PDF editor](https://pdf.net/blog/are-online-pdf-editors-safe) means trusting that service with your documents, at least temporarily. A few questions are worth answering before you upload anything.

1. **Is the connection encrypted?** Any credible online tool uses HTTPS, and government cybersecurity guidance recommends [encrypting data both at rest and in transit](https://www.cisa.gov/audiences/small-and-medium-businesses/secure-your-business/encrypt-business-data) as a baseline protection for sensitive files. If a tool’s URL doesn’t start with https://, don’t upload anything confidential.
2. **How long does the service keep your files?** Retention windows vary by tool and plan, from under an hour to 24 hours or more. For anything containing personal or proprietary information, a shorter, clearly stated window is safer.
3. **Can you lock the file down immediately after merging?** If the combined document is sensitive, password-protecting it right after the merge, in the same session, avoids a round trip of downloading and re-uploading.
4. **Do you need to keep the file long-term?** If it's a contract, invoice, or tax document, the [IRS accepts electronic records](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p583.pdf) as long as they stay accurate, complete, and accessible, so a merger that preserves formatting matters beyond just appearances.

## Best PDF Merger Tool FAQs

### #1. Is it better to merge PDFs online or offline?

Merging online works better for most people because it needs no installation and runs on any device. Offline merging, through tools like Preview or PDF Merger & Splitter, only makes sense when you’re combining sensitive files and want to avoid uploading them anywhere, or when you’re working without an internet connection.

### #2. Does merging PDFs reduce quality?

Merging PDFs does not reduce quality when a tool preserves the file’s original structure, which most tools we tested did. The exception was macOS Preview, whose weaker image optimization sometimes produced noticeably larger files after merging.

### #3. What is the best free PDF merger?

From our list, the best free PDF merger is iLovePDF, since it handled repeated merges in testing with no observed daily task limit and a 100MB per-task ceiling.
