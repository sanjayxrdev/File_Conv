Why Convert Excel to PDF?

You should convert Excel to PDF whenever you want to share, print, or archive a spreadsheet without letting anyone change its contents. A PDF locks your layout, numerical values, and formatting in place, so the file looks identical no matter which device or software opens it.

That said, here are the five main reasons to convert Excel to PDF:

  1. Preserve formatting. Converting an Excel spreadsheet to PDF keeps your fonts, colors, and cell borders exactly as designed, so a recipient's version of Excel never renders things differently.
  2. Prevent accidental edits. Once you export Excel to PDF, recipients cannot change your numbers, formulas, or layout unless you explicitly allow it.
  3. Simplify sharing. Instead of sending a workbook with multiple tabs, you deliver one static file that opens the same way on any device, including phones and tablets.
  4. Standardize printing. A PDF locks your print area and page breaks, so what you see on screen is exactly what prints, with no surprise blank pages or clipped columns.
  5. Support recordkeeping. Banks, HR departments, and government agencies widely accept PDF for invoices, reports, and applications, which makes converting your Excel file to PDF a practical step before submitting documents. If your spreadsheet contains payroll or financial data, consider password-protecting the PDF first.

How to Convert Excel to PDF on Mac

You can convert Excel to PDF on a Mac using the built-in Print dialog, without installing any extra software. Here's how to do it:

  1. Open your Excel workbook and select File > Print.
  2. In the Print dialog, click the PDF dropdown in the bottom-left corner.
  3. Select Save as PDF.
  4. Choose a file name and location, then click Save.

This method works for any app on macOS, not just Excel, and it keeps your Excel-to-PDF conversion from losing formatting, since macOS captures the exact print layout. Alternatively, you can use a PDF editor for Mac.

If your workbook has multiple sheets, macOS converts each visible sheet into its own page automatically. If you later need to edit the original numbers again, you can always convert the PDF back to Excel.

How to Save an Excel File as a PDF in Microsoft Excel

You can save an Excel file as a PDF directly from Excel's Export menu, and the steps work the same way on Windows and Mac. To do so, follow this process:

  1. Open your workbook in Excel and click File.
  2. Select Export, then choose Create PDF/XPS Document.
  3. Click Create PDF/XPS.
  4. Choose where to save the file, then click Publish.

Before exporting, check the Page Layout tab to confirm your print area and orientation, since Excel exports pages exactly as they would print. If you need to pull this data into a different format later, you can also convert the PDF to Word.

How to Convert Excel to PDF in Google Sheets

You can convert Excel to PDF in Google Sheets by uploading your workbook and using the built-in download option. Here's the process:

  1. Upload your Excel file to Google Drive and open it in Google Sheets.
  2. Click File, then hover over Download.
  3. Select PDF (.pdf).
  4. Adjust the print settings in the preview window, then click Export.

Google Sheets automatically opens XLSX files without a separate conversion step, so you can jump straight to downloading.

How to Keep Excel Formatting When Saving as PDF

Getting Excel to PDF without losing formatting comes down to four settings: margins, orientation, print area, and gridlines.

#1. Margins

Set your margins under Page Layout > Margins before converting, so tables and charts don't get pushed onto extra pages. Narrow margins usually work best for wide spreadsheets, while normal margins suit standard reports. Checking this setting first saves you from re-exporting the file later.

#2. Page Orientation

Wide spreadsheets often need landscape orientation to avoid columns spilling onto a second page. Switch this under Page Layout > Orientation, and preview the result before exporting so nothing gets cut off. If a page still comes out sideways after conversion, you can rotate the PDF pages afterward instead of starting the export over.

#3. Print Area

Define your print area under Page Layout > Print Area > Set Print Area to control exactly which cells appear in the PDF. Without this step, Excel may export blank rows or unrelated tabs along with your data, which is one of the most common formatting complaints after conversion.

#4. Gridlines

Decide whether to show or hide gridlines under the Sheet Options section of the Page Layout tab. Gridlines can make dense tables easier to read, but hiding them often looks cleaner for reports meant for clients or executives.

Is It Safe to Convert Excel Files Online?

Converting Excel files online is safe as long as you use a reputable tool that encrypts your data and deletes files after processing. Look for a converter that transmits files over HTTPS connections, since this protects information in transit between your browser and the server.

For bank statements or other sensitive figures, take a few extra precautions. Check the provider's privacy policy for a stated file deletion window, and avoid uploading financial data over public Wi-Fi.

In general, converting Excel to PDF online is no riskier than emailing the same file, and it's often safer, since sending unprotected spreadsheets by email carries its own risks.

How to Convert Excel to PDF FAQs

#1. Can I convert XLSX to PDF without Microsoft Excel?

Yes, you can convert XLSX to PDF without Microsoft Excel by using Google Sheets or an online converter like pdf.net. Both accept XLSX files directly and produce a PDF in a few clicks, so you don't need Excel installed on your device.

#2. Why does my Excel PDF cut off columns?

Your Excel PDF cuts off columns because the print area or page orientation wasn't set correctly before exporting. Switch to landscape orientation for wide spreadsheets and confirm your print area covers every column you want to include.

#3. Will charts and graphs appear in the PDF?

Yes, charts and graphs appear in the PDF as static images once you convert your spreadsheet. They keep their appearance, but they lose interactivity, so viewers can't hover over data points or edit the underlying values.
