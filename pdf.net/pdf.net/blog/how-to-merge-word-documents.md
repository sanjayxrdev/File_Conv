## Why Should You Merge Your Documents?

You should merge your documents because you can use them more efficiently and for more purposes afterward.

For instance, project managers can combine multiple deliverables to have a** uniform overview of results **at the end of a project. Merging Word documents is also essential for creating long [audit reports](https://www.ifad.org/elearning_cfs/beginners-guide-to-audit-reports.pdf), as they contain separate entities (e.g., the scope, the identified risks, etc.) that auditors should present at once.

Similarly, businesses can join Word files and have various **contracts, resumes, and forms **in one place. As these documents may refer to the same person, business plan, or entity, having them together can prevent excluding their essential parts.

Moreover, multiple individuals may need information from the same project, plan, or entity. In such cases, it is better to **combine documents **rather than keep numerous files in random places and hinder accessibility.

Another benefit of joining Word documents is that it can replace traditional bookbinding (if no physical copies are needed). So, instead of manually connecting pages from various books or documents, you can collect materials and use **digital tools to merge them**, which requires less time and effort.

## How to Merge Word Documents Step-by-Step in Microsoft Word

To merge Word documents using Microsoft Word or link another file to them, you can use an **insert text from a file **or** **a **drag-and-drop method**.

Let’s see these methods in greater detail:

### #1. Insert Text From File

Inserting text from a file in Word is a straightforward way to combine documents.

Here are the steps to completing this process:

1. Open Microsoft Word and select **Blank document**.

![Click the Insert menu in the top-left corner](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/i16te7yp/production/ef5977680045849c885ae5c78567b17ee2f131eb-664x190.png?fit=max&auto=format)

3. Choose the **Object** menu and select **Text from File**.  
![Choose the Object menu and select Text from File](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/i16te7yp/production/2e12d89197e0128f2235dd5623045e7db05047f7-664x204.png?fit=max&auto=format)

4. Find and select the file you want to add and click **Insert**.

### #2. Drag and Drop Method

With the drag-and-drop method, you cannot merge multiple DOCX files; you can only insert a hyperlink to another document. Still, this is a useful way to connect files without designated Word merge tools, by doing the following:

1. Run Microsoft Word and choose** Blank document**.  
![Drag and Drop Method for Merging Word Documents](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/i16te7yp/production/021f39b8f7ac661fe1e069d721b436d3028a8763-927x634.png?fit=max&auto=format)

2. Press the **Restore Down** button in the top-right corner to make the Word window smaller.  
![Press the Restore Down button to make the Word window smaller](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/i16te7yp/production/d6a38b7b1089c05cfba4a21cf4142abdcae3fa00-549x62.png?fit=max&auto=format)

3. Find the file you want to insert, click it, hold it, and drag it into the blank Word document.

4. Repeat this process for multiple files or select them all simultaneously if they are in the same location.

As mentioned, this method only adds the folder icon to your document, which you can double-click to open. To have the actual content on your Word document, you can use the **Insert Text from File **option.

## Why Convert Word Documents to PDF Before Merging?

You should convert Word documents to PDF and merge them to **enhance readability, have stable formatting, and facilitate sharing**.

Let’s learn more about these benefits:

### #1. Universal Readability

PDF format is highly compatible, meaning **all operating systems and devices support it**. Specifically, there are PDF readers for Windows, Mac, Android, and iOS, some of which are built-in and some of which you can download and install easily.

Similarly, you can use** online PDF readers** to access and use this file type. Plus, most programs and online software (e.g., pdf.net) allow you to edit your documents or change their size or format.

### #2. Locked Formatting

The formatting of PDFs is non-changeable in the sense that all devices that can open such files **show an identical document version**. This allows you to distribute PDFs without worrying whether the recipient can see the content in the original form.

Locked PDF formatting is also suitable for printing. This is because you may take the file to a copy shop or another place where people may prepare your document for printing using programs that differ from yours. In most cases, this scenario will change formatting for other documents (e.g., Word) **but not for PDF**.

### #3. Easier Sharing

Due to their uniform formatting, PDF documents are typically smaller than Word, Excel, and other files. The smaller the file is, **the easier it is to [share it](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-share-pdfs) on various platforms**. For example, you can have a lot of content in your PDFs and still not surpass the 25 MB limit for sharing using most email services.

Additionally, you can **upload low-sized documents faster **on social media channels or [cloud services](https://www.redhat.com/en/topics/cloud-computing/what-are-cloud-services) and open them easily on online platforms, as they can load quickly. Finally, your recipients can save time and download smaller PDFs within seconds.

## 3 Tips to Maintain Formatting When Merging

To prevent the disruption of formatting and have neat files after merging, pay attention to **styling and formatting techniques**.

Here are the most important styling and formatting methods to stick to:

- **Use a consistent style**. You should add headings and subheadings across your document. Instead of enlarging and bolding the font, as well as separating it from the rest of the text, you should **style your headings and level them logically** throughout the file.
- **Add [section breaks](https://www.computerhope.com/jargon/s/section-break.htm) where needed**. Just as page breaks make clear distinctions between pages within a section, section breaks help readers distinguish between the largest document entities. So, by adding them, you can make your file **more coherent**.
- **Standardize general formatting**. Use standard [typography](https://careerfoundry.com/en/blog/ui-design/beginners-guide-to-typography/) and ensure that your margins and spacing are set equally for all the pages and files you plan to merge. Generalized formatting will also reduce your document’s size (as non-standard fonts can be large) and make it easier to print (almost all devices and programs can read files with general formatting).

## How to Merge Word Documents FAQ

### #1. How can I merge Word documents with different headers?

You can merge Word documents with different headers **by inserting page breaks** into your document. This will create multiple sections in your file, with a new header and footer for each one, allowing for neat merging.

### #2. How do I merge Word documents without losing formatting?

You merge Word documents without losing formatting by converting Word files to PDFs and then [merging PDFs](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-merge-pdfs) using tools **such as pdf.net**. The conversion will create PDF files with locked formatting to sustain the combining process.

### #3. How do I insert a Word document into another Word document?

You insert a Word document into another Word document by **dragging and dropping** the file from your storage into another one, which you can open in MS Word. Also, you can use the **Insert text from File** feature in the same program to add one document to another.
