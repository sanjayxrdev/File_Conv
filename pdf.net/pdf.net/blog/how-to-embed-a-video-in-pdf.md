## Tips for Embedding Videos in PDFs

Keep these tips in mind when embedding a video in a PDF on Mac, Windows, etc.:

- **Use MP4 format. **Some video formats might play on your device but fail on someone else’s due to compatibility issues. Embedding MP4 in a PDF file is the safest choice because it is widely supported across platforms. It also ensures smooth playback without requiring extra plugins and maintains consistent quality. Meanwhile, you should avoid AVI, MOV, or WMV, as they tend to require specific codecs.
- **Keep video file size reasonable to avoid large PDFs. **Large videos can add to a PDF's size and make it slow to open and difficult to [share through email](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-email-large-pdf-file), as well as cause certain devices and readers to crash. You can compress it or shorten its length using a tool like the [Clideo video maker](https://clideo.com/video-maker), an online tool for editing and adjusting videos before embedding them in your PDF. It also helps to adjust the resolution; full 1080p is often unnecessary.
- **Test the PDF on multiple devices before sharing. **Each PDF reader handles embedded videos differently, and performance can vary across devices and operating systems. Therefore, you should open the PDF on multiple devices and readers and play the entire video each time to check for issues. Pay attention to the audio and any accompanying captions or interactive elements, too.
- **Add captions or instructions for video playback. **This way, your viewers can access the content without confusion. Captions and instructions improve [accessibility](https://pdf.net/blog/accessible-pdf) for those who are hard of hearing. Plus, it allows all viewers to follow along even if they watch muted; according to recent studies, [69% of consumers](https://www.forbes.com/sites/tjmccue/2019/07/31/verizon-media-says-69-percent-of-consumers-watching-video-with-sound-off/) watch videos with the sound off.

## 3 Common Issues When Embedding Videos in PDFs

Here are a few common issues when embedding videos in PDFs and how to troubleshoot them:

- **Videos are not playing in certain PDF readers. **Older readers or readers on mobile apps may fail to play videos, so to avoid playback problems, it is best to use the MP4 format and test your PDF on multiple readers before sharing. You should also [change your PDF permissions](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-change-pdf-permissions) and allow viewers to copy the videos’ URLs in case they aren’t clickable.
- **The PDF file size becomes too large.** Bloated PDFs can prompt your viewers to bounce, consume more storage space (especially when [embedded on websites](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-embed-a-pdf-in-a-website)), and lead to crashes or freezes on older devices. As such, you should make it a standard to [compress your PDFs](https://pdf.net/compress-pdf) and optimize your videos' lengths to keep your files manageable.
- **Compatibility problems with older software versions. **Many older readers and operating systems will not support the latest video codecs, which prevents the videos on your PDFs from playing. Interactive elements, like clickable links, may also be broken. To address this, you should include a plain-text link to the videos as a fallback.

## How to Embed a Video in PDF FAQs

### #1. Can I embed a YouTube video in a PDF?

**Yes**,** you can embed a YouTube video in a PDF** by using pdf.net. You can insert a textbox and paste the YouTube video link in it, or [add an image](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-insert-images-into-pdf) of the video’s thumbnail along with its URL. Additionally, it’s also possible to create a QR code that leads to the YouTube video and attach it as an image in your PDF.

### #2. Will the embedded video work on mobile devices?

Embedded videos **might** work on mobile devices if the **PDF reader supports** video playback and the video is in a widely compatible format like **MP4**. Performance depends on your device's **model** and **operating** **system** as well.

### #3. Can I edit or replace the video later?

**Yes**, **you can edit or replace the video later with our PDF editor**. You can return to the document and [**edit the text**](https://pdf.net/change-text-in-pdf) if you originally inserted a clickable link or **insert new images **to replace your old thumbnails or QR codes. Pdf.net allows you to store and organize your PDFs for as long as you need, so you won’t have to go looking for them or [make a new PDF](https://pdf.net/blog/how-do-i-make-a-pdf-file).

### #4. Does embedding a video increase PDF file size?

Yes, **embedding a video in a PDF will increase the file size**, and it might bloat your file significantly if it is **long**, in **high** **resolution**, or in a format like **AVI** or **MOV**. To ensure your document’s size stays reasonable, [compress your PDF](https://pdf.net/blog/compress-pdf-without-losing-quality) and optimize the video before embedding it.
