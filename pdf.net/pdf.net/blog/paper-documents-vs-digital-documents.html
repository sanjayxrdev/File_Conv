Digital vs. Paper Documents: Pros, Cons, & When to Choose Each

Learn about the differences between paper documents and digital documents, their pros and cons, and in which situations each should be used.

Marcus Cooper

Solutions Architect & Workflow Specialist

Table of contents

In an ongoing debate about paper documents and digital documents, the latter seem to be winning, as most companies, agencies, and organizations are transitioning to a digital format. The reason is that electronic documents are accessible, cost-effective, and take up significantly less space.

However, the benefits of paper documents can’t be overlooked, either, especially when they are still widely used. In many cases, they are still considered more official and safer than the digital ones.

In this article, we will compare paper documents vs. digital documents, discuss their pros and cons, and determine when it’s best to use each. We will also show you how to transition from paper to digital documents if you wish to modernize your office, drive digital adoption, and reduce clutter.

Key Takeaways

  • Key differences between digital and physical documents are security, storage and space, retrieval, searchability, collaboration options, environmental impact, and cost.
  • Paper documents are still often used for financial records and official documentation, like contracts, deeds, birth certificates, and more.
  • Meanwhile, digital documents are becoming the preferred document type because they are easy to access, share, and store.
  • If you want to transition from paper documents to digital documents, you need to prepare and organize your files, select a scanning method, pick a naming convention for your new digital documents, and upload them all to a cloud-based storage system.

What Are Paper Documents?

Paper documents are physical records created by writing or typing on paper, typically kept in filing cabinets or safes. Although their digitization is in full swing, they remain crucial for official documentation and financial records. As a result, many contracts, deeds, birth and death certificates, letters, and personnel files are still in paper form.

Pros of Paper Documents

Paper documents are still widely used because they come with several advantages. Here are some of those:

  • Immunity to cyberattacks. Paper documents exist in the physical, tangible space, so they can’t be targeted and accessed by hackers. Because of that, they are sometimes considered the safer option, though that depends on the security level.
  • No reliance on technology. Since these documents aren’t digitally stored, they can be opened even when the power is out or computer systems are down. For a similar reason, they are more reliable, as you’re not in danger of losing them due to a system failure.
  • “Wet” signatures. Physical signatures are often held in higher regard than digital or electronic ones, so many agencies and government departments still require them.
  • Acting as a physical backup. If something happens to computer systems and storage, paper documents of the contracts and deeds saved digitally can serve as a backup. This ensures that your documents aren’t lost, so it’s best to keep tangible copies of important records on hand.

Cons of Paper Documents

However, the disadvantages of paper documents can sometimes outweigh the benefits, which is why their digital versions are largely preferred. Let’s take a look at some of these cons:

  • Storage space. One of the most frequent problems with paper documents is storage, as they need to be kept in drawers and filing cabinets. So, if you have many, you may struggle to find space for them, and even when you do, they create a lot of clutter.
  • Physical damage. Paper documents may deteriorate over time or experience other kinds of damage that can affect their contents. Furthermore, they are vulnerable to fires, floods, and other types of disasters.
  • Negative environmental impact. The paper used for this type of document contributes to deforestation and waste.
  • No searchability. A well-organized archive of paper documents can make it easier to find what you’re looking for, but there’s no way to search for specific words or phrases within the document. This can make it difficult to look for particular information, and the entire process may take longer than necessary.

What Are Digital Documents?

Digital documents are files created to store specific information in an electronic format rather than on paper. As a result, these require digital devices, like laptops or smartphones, for viewing. Nowadays, they’re largely becoming the preferred format, so most people and organizations use PDFs, DOCX files, emails, and spreadsheets to store and share information.

Pros of Digital Documents

The many benefits of digital documents are the reason they are so widely used today. Here are a few of the most prominent ones:

  • Easy storage and organization. The management of digital documents is far simpler than that of paper files, as they are stored on electronic devices and organized in folders. In other words, there’s no need for large physical spaces and filing cabinets that take up most of the room. Plus, PDFs’ searchability makes it far easier to find what you’re looking for.
  • Options for collaboration. If specific settings are enabled, multiple users can view and edit digital documents simultaneously, which makes them indispensable for remote work. Pairing this with a live chat tool makes real-time team communication even smoother. This also saves the time it would take to deliver paper documents to each person who needs to access them.
  • Lower environmental footprint. Unlike paper documents, digital documents leave no waste and don’t contribute to deforestation, making them a more eco-friendly option.
  • Backup options. Electronic documents can be backed up to the cloud, which prevents data loss in case of a disaster. So, even if your devices are damaged and no longer functional, the documents are still intact and accessible.

Cons of Digital Documents

Despite their many advantages, digital documents do have a few significant downsides that are worth mentioning, such as:

  • Vulnerability to cyberattacks. Though encryption and password protection can significantly improve the security of digital documents, they are still at risk of cyberattacks. This is especially concerning for highly confidential documents containing private information.
  • Software compatibility issues. When opening digital documents, you may experience issues with software compatibility, which can result in difficulties accessing the file. These are easily resolved in most cases, but they may cause inconveniences, especially when you need to view the document urgently.
  • Potential storage issues. In general, easy storage is a big advantage, but it can become an issue over time. That’s because technology changes, software evolves, and many of the formats that used to be the norm become obsolete. As a result, documents that could be easily accessed before may no longer be accessible.
  • High initial costs. Transitioning to a digital format can be expensive at first, since these documents require significant investments in hardware and software. Also, the staff responsible for documentation and archiving needs to be trained to handle the transition, which may take up extra time and resources.

When It’s Best to Use Paper Documents

It’s best to use paper documents in specific situations, such as:

  • Legal and official records. Although electronic and digital signatures are just as valid as handwritten ones, many government agencies still require a wet signature on their records. So, wills, birth certificates, marriage certificates, property deeds, and similar documents are typically kept in paper form instead of being digitized.
  • Highly confidential data. If you believe there’s a risk of a cyberattack and data breach despite the protection methods you’ve implemented, it’s best to stick to paper documents. That’s especially the case with those containing highly confidential data, such as financial information.
  • Permanent records. Paper format still wins over digital when it comes to permanent storage, as technology changes very fast, and the current one may become obsolete in a few years. You can update your records whenever these changes happen, but it’s easier and safer to create a physical archive and keep permanent records there.
  • Areas with poor connectivity. If you live in an area with a poor internet connection or frequent power outages, digital documents aren’t an ideal solution. You’ll lose access to them as soon as the power’s out, so it’s better to use paper documents, at least as backups.

When You Should Use Digital Documents

Digital documents are becoming a staple, so you’ll be expected to use them in most situations. However, here are a few where they might play a bigger role:

  • Remote work. If you work remotely, most of the documentation you receive and handle will likely be digital, since this is far more convenient than using mail to send it back and forth. Plus, you’ll likely often collaborate with other team members on different types of documents, editing and annotating them when necessary.
  • Workplaces that emphasize efficiency. In some workplaces, it’s crucial to access documents or data within them as quickly as possible, in which case paper files aren’t particularly convenient. Digital documents are far easier to search, and the required information can be found within minutes.
  • Areas with limited storage space. If you’re in a small office that doesn’t have a large storage capacity, you should digitize your documents whenever possible. This allows you to save up space while still retaining access to all your files.
  • Secure backups. Since paper documents are vulnerable to damage and deterioration, you can use their digital versions as secure backups. However, make sure to implement strong enough protection to prevent potential data breaches.

How to Transition From Paper to Digital Documents

To transition from paper to digital documents, you need to organize your documents, select a scanning method, rename the digital files for easier access, and save them to a cloud-based storage system.

Now, we’ll take you through the process step by step:

#1. Organize Your Documents

If you have plenty of documents to convert from paper to a digital format, you need to sort them and choose which to start with. It might take time to complete the transition, so it’s best to begin with the most important ones, as well as decide whether you need to convert them all.

For instance, if you have any documents for permanent records, you don’t have to convert them to a digital form. As mentioned before, these are best kept as paper documents, though you can create electronic backups if it helps you feel safer.

#2. Select a Scanning Method

The most common ways to convert paper documents into digital documents are by scanning them with your phone or with an actual scanner. The former is typically used in informal settings when you only have a few documents to scan, while the latter is more suitable for companies with many files.

If you opt for your phone, you can use built-in features, like Google Drive for Android or Notes for iPhone. Both allow you to take a photo of the document with your camera and save it as a PDF or JPG.

However, if you choose to use a scanner, select a high-quality one and use Optical Character Recognition (OCR) software to make the scans searchable and editable. You may even partner up with a document scanning company that can convert all your paper documents into digital ones.

#3. Rename Digital Files

Once you have your PDF scans, make sure to use a consistent naming convention for your digital files and ensure they are all neatly organized into dedicated folders. That way, you’ll have an easier time retrieving them when you need to.

#4. Save Your Files to a Cloud-Based Storage System

Rather than keeping your files on a local device, upload them to Google Drive, Dropbox, or another cloud-based storage system to keep them safe and easily accessible. Even if your device malfunctions, you can retrieve your documents on a different one, as long as you have the right credentials.

Final Thoughts

Now that you understand the main differences between PDFs and printed documents, you can make a more informed decision about the format that suits your needs.

In general, we recommend digitizing your files to save storage space and enable easy access to them, no matter where you are. However, you should still make some room for physical copies and paper backups.

Paper Documents vs. Digital Documents FAQ

#1. Is digital better than paper?

Digital is better than paper when it comes to accessibility, storage, and organization, as these documents don’t take up physical space and can be easily searched. However, digital records are more vulnerable to cyberattacks, so they are less secure than paper ones.

#2. How do you convert paper documents into a digital format?

You can convert paper documents into a digital format by using a scanner to produce high-quality scans of the records in question. Then, you can use Optical Character Recognition (OCR) software to convert scanned images into searchable, editable digital text and finalize the process.

#3. Are digital documents more secure than paper documents?

Digital documents can be more secure than paper documents if proper protection methods have been implemented. For example, if your digital records use encryption, password protection, and access tracking, then they’re safer to use than paper documents. However, there’s always a risk of potential cyberattacks.
