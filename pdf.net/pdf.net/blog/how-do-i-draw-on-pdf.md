## 3 Other Ways to Draw on a PDF File

You can also draw on a PDF file using Mac’s Preview app, Microsoft Edge’s PDF editor, or mobile apps. Let’s take a closer look at these other methods:

### #1. Using Mac’s Preview App

If you want to draw on PDFs using a Mac device, you can do so with the pre-installed **Preview** app. Open the PDF in **Preview** and click the **Pen** icon to enable the **Markup** toolbar. From there, you can:

- Draw freehand with the **Sketch** or **Draw** tool; this is good for circling text, underlining points, or adding arrows
- **Highlight** text in different colors to draw attention to certain details
- Add **Shapes** to emphasize important sections. The blue handles let you resize, while the green handles let you reshape their forms. With these, you can also use the **Highlight** or **Zoom** tools to make it easier to locate key areas when reviewing your PDF
- Insert **Notes** and **Textboxes** for comments

This is a great alternative to drawing on PDFs online for free. You can use it when you are **short on time** or **don’t have an Internet connection.**

### #2. Using Microsoft Edge’s Built-in PDF Editor

Microsoft Edge’s built-in PDF editor lets you write on a PDF and add drawings. Open your PDF in it, find the toolbar at the top of your screen, and click **Add text **to [type new text](https://pdf.net/blog/how-do-i-type-on-a-pdf-file) in directly.

You can also start marking up your document with the **Pen** tool and **Highlight** tool. Alternatively, you can select the line of text you want to annotate, and then right-click and choose **Highlight** or **Add** **comment** from the drop-down menu that appears.

### #3. Using Mobile Apps

On iOS devices, you can use the **Files** app to draw on your PDFs. Simply open your document, tap the **Pen** icon to enable annotation mode, and use the **tools** that appear at the bottom of the screen.

There is a marker, a pen, a highlighter, and a pencil, and for each, you can adjust the stroke’s thickness, color, and opacity. If you make mistakes, the **Eraser** can help you undo them; once you are done, you can [share your PDF through email](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-email-large-pdf-file), Airdrop, or cloud storage.

On Android devices, many **free PDF editors**, such as Xodo, Adobe Acrobat, or Foxit PDF, have similar features. However, you need to download them first, and some may include ads or rather limited functionality that can slow you down.

## 4 Handy Tips for Drawing and Annotating PDFs Professionally

Keep the following tips in mind when drawing and annotating to ensure your documents remain polished and professional:

### #1. Use Consistent Colors for Clarity

Drawing or writing on PDFs in too many different colors can make your content look **confusing** or **distracting**.

If you don’t choose the right combinations and contrasts, you can also affect your [PDF’s **accessibility**](https://pdf.net/blog/accessible-pdf) and **readability**. For example, using red on green may be difficult for color‑blind readers to distinguish, and using gray on white can strain the eyes.

In contrast, using colors intentionally can be helpful in many ways:

- **You can establish a [visual structure](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/chapter/monograph/abs/pii/B9780124079144000038). **This improves the flow of reading and helps you draw attention to specific sections as needed.
- **You can create a color coding system.** This allows you to organize your ideas, [improve retention and recall with color cues](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-020-01058-w), and find important points or patterns while skimming and scanning more easily.
- **You can brand your documents. **Consistent use of certain colors can reinforce your personal or professional brand so that your work is instantly recognizable. This is essential if you are drawing on PDFs and then sharing or distributing them.

It is best to stick to just **three to four colors**, use them **consistently**, and verify them with an **online contrast checker **before finalizing your PDF.

### #2. Avoid Overcrowding Pages With Markings

If your PDF is already text-heavy, marking it up further can **cause clutter**; your content will then be difficult to read and can create **cognitive overload**. This counteracts all the benefits of adding annotations and drawings to your document in the first place.

Therefore, it is always important to **be selective and strategic**. You can [insert new pages](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-add-pages-to-pdf) to your PDF to create more room or [edit the text](https://pdf.net/change-text-in-pdf) directly to avoid taking up too much space. Also, don’t forget to re**view your PDFs on mobile devices** before sharing them; a page might appear crowded on a phone while looking fine on a laptop.

### #3. Combine Drawings With Highlights or Comments for Context

Using drawings along with highlights and comments can make [your annotations](https://pdf.net/annotate-pdf) **clearer**. For example, you can draw an arrow to point to a specific sentence and add a comment explaining why it is important.

This way, when you **refer back to your PDF **later on, you won’t have to rack your brain trying to remember what each mark means. It also helps anyone else reading the document **understand** your notes.

You should never draw directly on your original PDF, as you might need to duplicate it or edit it in the future. Instead, **duplicate the document** and make all your annotations, highlights, or drawings on that version.

## Do You Lose Quality When Drawing on a PDF?

**You generally don’t lose quality when drawing on a PDF**. PDFs are vector-based, so annotations like highlights or shapes can be added without compromising the original content.

However, if you **export the PDF** to formats like JPEG, PNG, or DOCX, the document may be compressed, which reduces clarity. If you **“print” a PDF** to create a new document, some printers or drivers may also rasterize or compress the content, which affects the quality.

To minimize the chances of this happening, you should** limit conversions **between PDF and other formats. **Before sharing or printing, **be sure to preview by zooming in on all the drawings and ensuring that they are crisp.

## How do I Draw on PDF FAQs

To edit a PDF with a pencil, you can use the **Files** app on iOS mobile devices. If you are not on a phone or a tablet, you can use **pdf.net’s **marker and reduce the stroke’s thickness to mimic that of a pencil.
