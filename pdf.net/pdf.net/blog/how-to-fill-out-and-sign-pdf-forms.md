- Standardizing your team's signature files and placement rules cuts down on formatting errors and speeds up turnaround.

## Fillable PDF vs. Flat PDF: What Changes For You?

Here’s the difference between fillable and flat PDFs and what the choice between the two changes for you:

- A **fillable PDF** (also called an interactive PDF form) contains embedded form fields created in a PDF authoring tool. When you open one in a [PDF editor](https://pdf.net/blog/best-pdf-editors) or viewer, you see clickable boxes, drop-down menus, checkboxes, and radio buttons. Typing goes directly into those fields.
- A **flat PDF** (sometimes called a static or non-interactive PDF) has no embedded form structure. The form layout exists as a fixed image or text layer, but there are no fields a user can click into. This includes scanned paper forms, printed-then-scanned PDFs, and documents where the author deliberately flattened or locked the fields.

Common symptoms of a flat PDF include:

- Clicking anywhere on the form produces no cursor or text entry.
- A PDF viewer opens the file but shows no highlighted field boxes.
- Text you place manually appears in the wrong position or at an unexpected size.

For flat PDFs, the fix is to use a PDF editor's free-form text tool to place text boxes precisely over each blank area. If alignment is difficult, zoom in to the area you are filling and resize the text to match the surrounding printed text size. pdf.net's tool that [adds text to a PDF](https://pdf.net/add-text-to-pdf) gives you control over placement, font, and size without requiring the document to have pre-built fields.

## 4 Common Issues and How to Fix Them

Fill-and-sign jobs can also hit snags, which are usually tied to **file size, formatting, or a locked document**. Here's how to solve the ones that come up most often.

- **Signature won't fit the box.** Resize the signature element by dragging a corner handle inward. If the signature image has excess white space around it, trim that space using an image editor or a signature background remover before uploading.
- **Text fields don't line up with the form.** This usually means the PDF is a flat document being filled with free-form [text boxes](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-add-text-box-to-pdf). Zoom into the page to at least 100% before placing text so placement is precise. Also verify the page scaling setting in your browser is at 100%, not a larger or smaller zoom that could shift your perception of positioning.
- **Can't edit or type in the PDF at all.** First, determine whether the document is fillable or flat (see the section above). If it is fillable but still unresponsive, the document may be password-protected or have editing restrictions applied. In that case, you may need to [unlock the PDF](https://pdf.net/unlock-pdf) before proceeding.
- **File too large to upload.** Large files slow down uploads and may exceed platform limits. For oversized files, use a [PDF compressor](https://pdf.net/compress-pdf) to reduce file size before uploading. If a scanned document is the culprit, reducing image resolution within the scan (300 DPI is [typically sufficient for legibility](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-3-Implementation-Resources/63A/resolution/)) will produce a smaller file. Alternatively, [split the PDF](https://pdf.net/split-pdf) into sections and handle each part separately.

## How Teams Can Standardize the Fill-and-Sign Process

Teams can standardize the fill-and-sign process by:

- **Creating a shared signature library.** Store each team member's approved signature image (transparent PNG format) in a shared folder. When a document needs to be signed, anyone can retrieve the correct file rather than creating a new version.
- **Defining placement conventions.** For documents your team signs regularly, such as [employment letters](https://www.rmu.edu/sites/default/files/user_files/employment-letters%20%2019-20.pdf), vendor agreements, and [NDAs](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-sign-an-nda), document where the signature should be placed, what size it should be set to, and whether initials are required on additional pages.
- **Using shared links for faster return.** For documents that need signatures from multiple parties, a signature request workflow via email or shareable link removes the back-and-forth of emailed attachments. Our [signature request tool](https://pdf.net/request-signature-pdf) allows you to assign signing fields to recipients, who can then sign directly from a link without creating an account.
- **Treating the downloaded file as final**. Once a document is signed and downloaded, avoid reopening it for further edits. Any change to a signed document after the fact complicates the evidentiary value of the signature. Rename the downloaded file clearly (e.g., “contract_signed_2026-07-22.pdf”) and store it in a location that preserves version history.

## How to Fill Out and Sign PDF Forms FAQ

### #1. Is typing my name on a PDF legally valid?

Typing your name on a PDF **can count as a legally valid signature** in most U.S. commercial contexts, according to [15 U.S.C. § 7001 of the federal E-SIGN Act](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7001). Validity depends on more than the typed name itself; namely, both parties must have agreed to sign electronically, and the signed record needs to be retained and traceable back to the signer.

### #2. Can I sign a scanned PDF without printing?

Yes, **you can sign a scanned PDF without printing**, as most scanned documents are flat, non-interactive files. You can open it in a browser-based PDF editor, use the text tool to fill in any required fields, then add your signature using the typed, drawn, or uploaded method. No printing is involved at any point.

### #3. How do I get a signed copy back from another party?

You can get a signed copy back from another party **through the signature platform, email, or a shared link**. If you sent the document via a signature request tool, the platform notifies you when the recipient has signed and makes the completed file available to download. If you emailed the document directly, ask the recipient to download the signed version from their session and return it by email or shared link. pdf.net's [tool for PDF sharing](https://pdf.net/share-pdf) lets you generate a secure link to the completed file instead of sending email attachments.
