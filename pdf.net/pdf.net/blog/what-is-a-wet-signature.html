What Is a Wet Signature? Meaning, Importance, and Use Cases

A wet signature is a handwritten mark made on a physical document using a pen or stamp. Discover when you need one and when you can avoid it.

Sarah Martinez

Senior Product Manager

Table of contents

A wet signature is a physical mark on a document made with a pen, seal, or another identification mark or “wet ink,” hence the name. This is the opposite of the meaning of a “dry” signature: an umbrella term used to describe electronic signatures that use no ink and, therefore, require no time to dry.

A wet signature used to be a binding contract, and it still is in some cases. However, with the advancement of technology, electronic signatures have become more and more accepted as a faster and more secure alternative.

In this article, we’ll discuss the use of wet signatures, drawing a parallel between them and e-signs and digital signatures. We’ll also explain how the concept of a wet signature is applied online. Let’s begin!

Key Takeaways

  • A wet signature is a handwritten signature on a physical document, traditionally used for legal and formal validation.
  • Wet signatures are still mandatory for certain legal documents, regulated industries, and non-digital signers, depending on jurisdiction.
  • Electronic signatures (e-signs) offer a faster, more convenient, and secure alternative for signing documents remotely.
  • Digital signatures add encryption and authentication to e-signs, providing higher security and integrity for sensitive or high-risk documents.
  • Tools like pdf.net allow users to sign PDFs online, streamlining the signing process without printing or scanning.

What Is a Wet Signature?

A wet signature represents a handwritten signature on a physical piece of paper, be it an agreement, contract, or proposal (a wet-signed document). It goes under various names, including the following:

  • Wet ink signature
  • Handwritten signature
  • Physical signature
  • Wet sign
  • Pen and ink signature
  • Manual (manually written) signature

The term “wet” dates back to when ink was used for such purposes more commonly—namely, it took some time for it to dry on paper.

In addition to a handwritten signature, wet signature examples traditionally included several other forms of authentication, such as a personal seal, stamp, and thumbprint. All of these models were (and still are) legal signatures used to verify the validity of documents and confirm identification.

How to Obtain a Wet Signature on a Document?

The process of obtaining a wet signature goes as follows:

  1. Prepare the document that needs to be signed.
  2. If the document is in digital form, print it out on paper.
  3. Sign the document in the designated area with a pen (or ink).
  4. Have a witness be present during signing if legal requirements call for one.
  5. Once the document is signed, you can mail it, hand-deliver it, or scan it and send it via email.

When Is a Wet Signature Still Required?

Wet signature is still required in legal and formal contexts, although electronic signatures have become more common over the past few decades. Let’s examine in more detail when a wet signature is mandatory.

Legal Documents That Require a Wet Signature

Many legal documents, particularly high-stakes ones that must be notarized, still require wet signatures to confirm their validity. These include the following:

  • Wills and testaments
  • Trust agreements
  • Deeds
  • Property transfers
  • Sworn statements
  • Affidavits
  • Court orders
  • Adoption papers
  • Marriage licenses
  • Passports
  • Immigration paperwork
  • Promissory notes

For some of these documents, a witness must be present during the signing to confirm the signer’s identity. In that case, a wet signature is still a must, but others aren’t quite as rigid about their requirements. Ultimately, it depends on the jurisdiction; some states are beginning to allow digital signatures as well.

Industries That Still Require Wet Signatures

Industries that still require wet signatures are those using the above-mentioned high-stakes documents that usually must be notarized or verified in person. For example, law, government, finance, healthcare, and real estate frequently rely on handwritten signatures instead of digital or electronic ones.

Non-Digital Signers

It’s important to mention that, even when the e-signing option is available and encouraged, there are people who rely on wet signatures anyway. This is usually because they don’t have access to the required technology or aren’t comfortable using it if they do. For instance, the elderly often prefer to sign their documents in writing because it’s more of a hassle to learn how to do it digitally.

What Are Electronic Signatures?

An electronic signature, or e-sign, is a digital version of someone’s handwritten signature. As an alternative to a wet signature, it’s commonly used for signing documents online. Its popularity and wide use lie in the fact that it allows for remote signing, thus eliminating the need for physical paperwork.

This type of signature offers a convenient way of signing a wide range of documents, including forms, agreements, and contracts. However, while e-signs are generally legally valid, not every jurisdiction recognizes them as such; in some cases, you may still need to sign a document by hand.

When Are E-Signatures Better than Wet Signatures?

E-signatures are better than wet signatures when:

  • You need quick signing: Speed can be a factor in signing certain documents, and this is where e-signs come in handy. They’re completed instantly, as there are no delays from printing and mailing.
  • Parties are in different locations: A document may need to be signed by a person in one city or country and sent to a person in another location. In such cases, an electronic signature is a great choice because it allows you to request a signature online and eliminates the need for shipping.
  • There are lots of documents: Businesses typically process large volumes of forms and contracts. Luckily, e-signs can streamline the workflow, thus saving time.
  • You need a secure signing process: Electronic signatures provide security measures such as encryption or two-factor authentication, which are especially important when signing documents containing sensitive information.
  • It’s important to reduce the cost of the process: Wet signatures imply printing, mailing, and storing documents, all of which cost money. E-signs, on the other hand, save money by eliminating those extra steps.
  • You’re working digitally: If you create and store documents digitally, converting them to physical files takes time. For this reason, it’s better to sign them electronically.
  • Environmental impact matters: Printing physical documents requires paper and energy use. In contrast, e-signs save those resources, contributing to a more environmentally friendly workflow.

When choosing which one to use, you must consider all these factors, as well as specific requirements in your jurisdiction or the organization issuing the document.

Are Electronic Signatures as Safe as Wet Signatures?

Basic electronic signatures may offer similar or slightly better security than wet signatures, while digital signatures provide significantly higher security due to encryption and authentication. The adoption rates are significant as well, with advanced electronic signatures leading the U.S. digital signatures market with 48.38% revenue share in 2025.

Establishing the validity of a wet signature requires comparing copies of the signature and offering testimony from witnesses who were present during the signing or handwriting experts. On top of being time- and money-consuming, this process can be unreliable due to the human factor.

In contrast, advanced electronic signatures use advanced technology to enhance security. They often come with encryption, audit trails, and various authentication methods to confirm the signer’s identity and prevent tampering. Thanks to these digital safeguards, it’s fairly easy to track when and where a certain document was signed.

Simply put, wet signatures rely on subjective methods to prove authenticity. Meanwhile, e-signs (and especially digital signatures) leverage objective systems that leave little room for error. With that in mind, not only do electronic signatures save time and money, but they also reduce the risk of fraud.

3 Key Downsides of Wet Signatures

The downsides of wet signatures include higher costs, longer turnaround time, limited privacy and security, and record-keeping issues. Let’s examine them more closely:

#1. Cost and Turnaround Time

Wet signatures are money-consuming, and not just because of the cost of printing and shipping (especially in the case of an overnight delivery). If you’re running a business, you also need to take into account the cost of lost revenue when it takes more than a week to receive a signed document.

This is where it also becomes evident how time-consuming wet signatures can be. Not only are you at the mercy of delivery services as it is, but you also need to factor in weekends and holidays when waiting for a document. Needless to say, if your sales contract takes too long to arrive, your potential client may explore other options in the meantime.

#2. Privacy and Security

Handling sensitive information calls for high levels of privacy and security, which, unfortunately, can be compromised in the case of physical documents. They can get stolen or lost, and even if they don’t, it’s still difficult to control who has access to them.

Electronic signatures, or, more precisely, digital signatures, involve no such risks. They offer numerous security methods to verify the signer’s identity, as well as the identities of those who can gain entry to the documents.

For instance, this is what audit trails are for. They track precisely who has viewed, signed, and accessed the document and list time stamps and IP addresses. As a result, both parties will be notified if an unauthorized individual gains access or tries to make changes, which allows them to act accordingly.

#3. Record-Keeping Challenges

When a document is signed with a wet signature and sent back to your company, it has to be scanned and stored in the cloud or on a local drive.

This can pose a problem if any of the parties need to access the physical version of the document again. Namely, it takes much longer to find it in boxes and filing cabinets in storage than it does to pull it up on a computer.

How to Convert a Wet Signature to a Digital Format

You can convert a wet signature to a digital format following these steps:

  1. Sign a blank, unlined piece of paper with a black pen. The contrast between the background and the signature should be as high as possible.
  2. Use your phone camera or a scanner app to take a photo of the signature. Make sure it’s clear and well-lit.
  3. Crop the picture to remove the unnecessary white background and increase the brightness and contrast in your image editing app.
  4. If possible, use image editing software like Photoshop to remove the background completely and make it transparent.
  5. Save the image in a PNG format if the background is transparent. Otherwise, JPG is also acceptable.
  6. Add this image to electronic documents using an online tool.

Final Thoughts

Despite the growing reliance on electronic and digital signatures, a wet signature still has its place in certain fields and situations. That’s why it’s important to know what a wet signature is and when it should be used.

However, it’s also crucial to be aware of its limitations, such as its security risks or inconvenience. Understanding both the pros and the cons of wet signatures will help you choose the right signing method for your particular situation and streamline the entire process.

What Is a Wet Signature? FAQs

#1. Can a wet signature be electronic?

A wet signature can be transferred to electronic form if you scan it and convert it to digital format. This process allows the physical signature to be used in electronic documents, making it a convenient solution for digital transactions.

#2. Can a wet signature be scanned?

Yes, a wet signature can be scanned with a scanner or mobile device’s camera to create its digital copy. This digital version can then be used in electronic documents, enabling secure digital transactions and recordkeeping.

#3. Are electronic signatures legal?

Electronic signatures are legal, although some formal documents, such as wills, adoption papers, and court orders, still require wet signatures.

#4. Can I create a wet signature in Word?

You can create a wet signature in Word by signing on a piece of paper, taking a picture of the signature, uploading it to your computer, and inserting the image in a Word file.

#5. Is a scanned wet signature legally valid?

Yes, a scanned wet signature is as legally valid as any other electronic signature, but it offers low security since it can easily be tampered with. Practically anyone can remove it from the document using the right software, and your scanned signature can end up in the wrong hands if you’re not careful.

#6. Does a wet signature need to be in blue or black ink?

A wet signature can be either in blue or black ink, depending on the specific document requirements. Always make sure to check before signing, but when in doubt, choose blue ink; it’s the easiest way to distinguish your original signature from photocopies.

#7. Can you use a wet signature on a digital document?

You can use a wet signature on a digital document if you print it, sign it by hand, then scan or photograph it to create an image. This process involves quite a few extra steps, though, and it’s generally unnecessary considering the number of available options for adding an e-signature.
