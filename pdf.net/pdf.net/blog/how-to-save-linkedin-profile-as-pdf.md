## Key Takeaways

- Saving your LinkedIn profile as a PDF helps you quickly share your professional summary, apply for jobs, or keep a backup before making changes.
- You can download it directly from LinkedIn using the “Save to PDF” option on desktop or mobile, though it works best when your profile language is set to English.
- If the built-in tool fails, clearing your browser cache, switching browsers, or printing the profile to PDF manually can fix the issue.
- Alternative methods include copying your profile to Word and saving it as a PDF, using LinkedIn-integrated resume builders, or taking screenshots and combining them.

## Why Save Your LinkedIn Profile as a PDF?

You might want to save your LinkedIn as a PDF for a few practical reasons. For example, it is helpful when **applying for jobs **that ask for a resume, as it allows you to quickly share a professional summary without starting from scratch. It also comes in handy for **networking** or **offline sharing **when you need to send your details by email or present them at an event.

Moreover, downloading your LinkedIn profile as a PDF helps you **keep a personal record **of your experience, skills, and milestones. You can use it to track your career growth or refer back to past versions over time.

It is also a smart **backup** option; before making changes to your profile, you can download a copy to have peace of mind in case something goes wrong.

## How to Save a LinkedIn Profile as a PDF on Desktop

To save your LinkedIn profile as a PDF on desktop, follow these five simple steps:

1. Visit LinkedIn and log in to your account.

2. Select **Me** from the toolbar at the top of the page and select **View Profile**.

![Select Me from the toolbar at the top of the page and select View Profile.](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/i16te7yp/production/77e66beca607d1bd153ebcfc78d631131ac08da5-1151x351.png?fit=max&auto=format)

3. Click **Resources** under the **Connections** line.

![Click Resources under the Connections line](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/i16te7yp/production/3654d251127a1cee125a1bc93ef48fa84adfe1a5-818x300.png?fit=max&auto=format)

4. Select **Save to PDF **from the drop-down menu that appears.

You will get a notification like this in the bottom-left corner of your screen once your download starts:

![Select Save to PDF from the drop-down menu that appears](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/i16te7yp/production/522ec2f58d72c48989ad797e33f127556564e020-412x140.png?fit=max&auto=format)

5. Locate the downloaded LinkedIn profile on your computer.

However, please note that this will **only work properly with English text**. To use it without issues, both the content and the account’s language must be set to English. If your profile or settings use a different language, the PDF may [miss some text](https://pdf.net/blog/pdf-missing-text).

## How to Save Someone Else’s LinkedIn Profile as a PDF

Saving someone else’s LinkedIn profile as a PDF comes with a few limitations and considerations. In most cases, the option is only available for your [**1st-degree connections**](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a545636/your-network-and-degrees-of-connection), meaning you need to be directly connected to the person.

Even then, LinkedIn restricts access based on the user’s privacy settings, so you might not be able to [generate a PDF ](https://pdf.net/blog/how-do-i-make-a-pdf-file)file for every profile. It is also important to **consider the ethical side** before saving or sharing someone else's information.

Just because the option exists does not mean it is always appropriate to use it. Always respect privacy, avoid sharing personal details without permission, and use the information responsibly. Also, you should note that saving others’ LinkedIn profiles offline is** limited to 100 downloads per month.**

Follow these steps to download other LinkedIn profiles:

1. Open the LinkedIn profile that you want to download as a PDF.

2. Select **More**, which is next to **Message**.

![Select More, which is next to Message](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/i16te7yp/production/a01099725fd50dc6bb2ed1df4b3a14d810467373-527x438.png?fit=max&auto=format)

3. Click **Save to PDF **in the drop-down menu that pops up.

## Troubleshooting: LinkedIn “Save as PDF” Not Working

If LinkedIn’s **Save as PDF** feature is not working, there are a few simple steps you can try to fix the issue.

- **Clearing your browser cache and cookies** is a good place to start; this can usually resolve glitches.
- If that does not help, **change to another browser** to see if the problem is browser-specific. LinkedIn works well on Chrome, Firefox, and Safari, so it is worth testing on a few.
- Next, check your LinkedIn settings; as previously mentioned, the feature may not work if your profile and account language aren’t **set to English**. Also, take a look at your privacy settings; if **your profile visibility** is restricted, LinkedIn might block you from generating a PDF.
- If you are using LinkedIn on your phone or tablet, you should ensure it is** updated to the latest version** to access all features and avoid potential bugs. As a last resort, you can manually save a profile by printing it to PDF. Simply open the profile, press **Ctrl+P** on Windows or **Command+P** on a Mac, select **Save as PDF** as your printer, and download the file.

## How to Save LinkedIn Profile as a PDF FAQs

### #1. Can I save LinkedIn posts in PDF format?

**You can’t save LinkedIn posts in [PDF format](https://pdf.net/blog/what-does-pdf-format-mean) directly**, but you can use your browser’s **Print to PDF** feature to save individual posts. Just open the post, press Ctrl+P or Command+P on Mac, and select **Save as PDF**.

### #2. Can I save my LinkedIn profile as a PDF without logging in?

No, **you can’t save your LinkedIn profile as a PDF without logging in**. The **Save to PDF **feature is located on your profile and is only accessible after you have signed in to your account, as LinkedIn requires authentication to protect user data.

### #3. Is saving someone’s profile allowed?

Saving another person’s profile **is allowed for 1st-degree connections in most cases**, but only if their privacy settings permit it. However, you should always consider ethical use and avoid sharing or distributing their information without consent.

### #4. Why can't I save my LinkedIn profile to PDF?

You might not be able to save your LinkedIn profile to PDF because of **browser issues**, **outdated app versions**, or **incorrect language settings**. Try switching browsers, updating LinkedIn, or using workarounds like copying to Word and then converting the file to PDF.
