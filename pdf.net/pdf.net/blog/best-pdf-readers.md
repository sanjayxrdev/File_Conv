What you should look for in a PDF reader depends on how you use PDFs day-to-day. - Easy way to upload documents

## 4 Benefits of Using PDF Readers

More teams now work from outside a traditional office; namely, [22.6% of U.S. workers teleworked in March 2026](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/22-6-percent-of-workers-teleworked-in-march-2026.htm), per the Bureau of Labor Statistics. PDF readers are important for remote teams because they keep documents consistent no matter who or where opens them.

Below are the top four benefits of using PDF readers:

1. **More accurate. **Unlike web browsers and word processors that often reflow text, PDF readers [preserve the exact layout](https://www.loc.gov/preservation/digital/formats/) to make documents easier to read and understand. This makes them critical for exams, manuals, legal documents, and official forms.
2. **Better built-in features.** Image viewers and browser previews usually only allow scrolling and zooming. PDF readers offer more — thumbnails, bookmarks, and reading modes that help you move through long documents efficiently.
3. **Faster processing with complex files. **PDF readers are designed to handle large documents with images, vectors, and embedded fonts. They don't encounter lagging, crashing, or rendering problems that are common with Word processors and browsers.
4. **Reliable form handling.** One of the most common uses of a PDF is completing forms. With PDF readers, you can properly interact with dropdowns, checkboxes, and signature lines. Word processors often break form functionality or fail to save entered data.

## Final Thoughts

Before you settle on a PDF reader, decide whether you mainly need to read PDFs quickly or want to edit them along the way. It also helps to think about how often you handle PDFs and whether you're doing it as part of a team.

Businesses usually choose Adobe Acrobat, Foxit, or Nitro PDF for e-signature and document-approval workflows, while everyday users opt for pdf.net, Smallpdf, Pdfgear, or SumatraPDF.

If you want a fast, simple, and secure solution, pdf.net is hard to beat: it is fully browser-based, easy to use, and does not require registration, so you can start reading — or editing — the moment you open a PDF.

## Best PDF Readers FAQs

### #1. Is there a better PDF reader than Chrome?

pdf.net is a better PDF reader than Chrome if you want to open and edit documents in a single browser tab. For example, you can review an [invoice](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-make-a-pdf-invoice) and sign it, or fill out a [PDF form](https://pdf.net/pdf-forms) before sending it off.

### #2. Can I use a PDF reader online without downloading software?

Yes, you can use a PDF reader online without downloading software through browser-based options like pdf.net and Smallpdf. These save time since there is nothing to install, though you will need a reliable internet connection.

### #3. Which PDF reader is best for Windows?

PDF-XChange and SumatraPDF are built specifically for Windows. pdf.net also works on Windows because it is fully browser-based and is not tied to one operating system.

### #4. Are there PDF readers for Mac that are free?

Yes, there are [free PDF readers for Mac](https://pdf.net/blog/best-pdf-editor-for-mac). pdf.net is one of the top options since it is browser-based — no download required — and it scored 5/5 for navigation ease in our testing. You will not need to pay unless you want to edit your document.

### #5. Can I annotate and edit PDFs with a PDF reader?

Yes, most PDF readers let you annotate and edit PDFs. SumatraPDF is the one exception on this list: in our tests, it was the only tool with zero annotation tools of any kind — no highlighting, comments, drawing, sticky notes, or signatures.
