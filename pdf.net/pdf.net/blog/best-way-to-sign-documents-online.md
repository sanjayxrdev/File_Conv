## What Does “Signing Documents Online” Actually Mean?

“Signing documents online” usually refers to both [electronic signatures and digital signatures](https://pdf.net/blog/difference-between-electronic-and-digital-signature). However, they aren’t exactly the same.

An electronic signature, or e-signature, is any** electronic way of showing intent to sign** a document; for example:

- Typing your name at the end of an email
- Drawing your signature with a mouse
- Clicking “I agree” on a website
- Inserting an image of your signature into a document

It is easier to dispute than a [digital signature](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389064454_A_Comprehensive_Study_of_Digital_Signatures_Algorithms_Challenges_and_Future_Prospects), which offers robust** identity verification and tamper detection**. Therefore, it is considered more secure and is commonly used for **legal, financial,** and **compliance documents.**

Both types of signatures can be legally valid under the [ESIGN Act](https://pdf.net/blog/what-is-esign-act) and UETA in the United States. They regulate and recognize these signatures’ legality as long as there is affirmative consent from the signer and reliable recordkeeping of signed documents that are legible and reproducible.

However, certain documents, such as wills, [codicils](https://www.legalandgeneral.com/insurance/over-50-life-insurance/guides/what-is-a-codicil/), and testamentary trusts, may require [wet signatures](https://pdf.net/blog/what-is-a-wet-signature) or additional formalities depending on state law, so electronic signing is not universally accepted for these documents.

In the context of signing documents online, the [PDF format](https://pdf.net/blog/what-does-pdf-format-mean) is the most common because it preserves formatting and is widely supported across devices and platforms.

## 4 Tips for Secure Online Signing

To sign documents online securely, keep these four tips in mind:

1. **[Password-protect documents](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-password-protect-a-pdf) before or after signing. **This helps prevent unauthorized viewing, editing, or tampering once the document is sent. If you need to share the password with someone else, you should do so through a separate channel, preferably a call.
2. **Avoid signing documents on public Wi-Fi. **These connections are often unsecured and can [expose your documents](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/386142859_The_Risks_of_Public_Wi-Fi_and_How_to_Stay_Safe) to interception. If you need to sign something right away, it is best to use a trusted private network or a mobile hotspot instead.
3. **Store your signed files in protected folders to reduce the risk of unauthorized access. **This is especially important if your device is shared, misplaced, or compromised. You should set your security settings carefully and apply encryption to further [protect your e-signatures](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-protect-e-signature-in-pdf).
4. **Save a copy of the signed document and any confirmation for your records.** This helps you verify what was signed and provides proof if questions or disputes come up later. It also supports compliance with e-signature laws, such as the ESIGN Act.

Online e-signatures are legally binding under the ESIGN Act, UETA, and [ESRA](https://its.ny.gov/electronic-signatures-and-records-act-esra-regulation) in the U.S. if they meet legal requirements such as **clear intent to sign, consent from all parties, **and** proper record retention.**

**Yes, you can sign a PDF on your phone**. Pdf.net is a good option to do so since it’s fully browser-based. You can upload, sign, and save documents directly from your phone or tablet’s browser without downloading an app.

It is safe to sign documents online when you **use trusted tools, secure Internet connections, **and **basic file protection practices. **Therefore, you should avoid public Wi-Fi, protect your signed documents, and retain copies for your records to reduce risk.
