In this article, we will explain the main reasons for removing watermarks, show you four safe methods to do so, and share alternative approaches to hiding or minimizing them. Additionally, we will touch on when watermark removal is not appropriate.

## Reasons for Removing a Watermark From a PDF

There are several reasons for removing a watermark from a PDF.

- **Updating documents for meetings. **If you are updating documents for a meeting with clients or colleagues, you may want to remove watermarks that clash with your new layout.
- **Studying or taking notes. **Watermarks can make it hard to read, annotate, or highlight sections, so knowing how to remove them can be helpful.
- **Changing or removing branding. **If your PDFs include watermarks as branding, or if your branding has changed, you may want to remove them before sharing your document.

However, PDF watermark removal is **not** always appropriate. For example, you shouldn't remove watermarks from copyrighted, confidential, or licensed documents, especially if you intend to redistribute them. With these types of materials, watermarks serve as a protection for intellectual property, so tampering with them might mean **violating [copyright laws](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/).**

Having this in mind, check the usage rights or obtain permission from the creators of the PDFs before making any modifications, unless you are removing watermarks for personal, educational, or internal use.

## How to Remove a Watermark from a PDF: 3 Easy Methods

You can remove a watermark from a PDF using desktop PDF editors, online PDF editors, Microsoft Word, or recreate the PDF without a watermark. Let’s take a closer look at each method below.

### #1. Desktop PDF Editors

Popular editors you can use to remove a watermark from a PDF include Adobe Acrobat, Foxit PDF Editor, and Nitro PDF. The removal steps will vary from editor to editor; it usually goes similarly to this:

1. Install the editor.
2. Open your PDF in the editor.
3. Go to the **Tools** menu and select **Edit** **PDF**.
4. Click **Watermark > Remove**.
5. Save the document and download it in PDF format.

Depending on the desktop PDF editor you choose, you may have to** pay for a plan, **which is usually subscription-based and requires a monthly/annual commitment. If you just need to do a one-off edit, all three options above offer free trials you can take advantage of.

Using desktop PDF editors is the preferred method if your PDF contains **sensitive** information or if you want an **offline** solution that preserves your formatting, fonts, and graphics.

### #2. Cropping or Redacting

If the watermark appears only on the edges or corners of a page, cropping or [redacting](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/r/redacted.asp) the affected area can also work. Most PDF editors have a **Crop** tool that you can use to get this done. For more targeted adjustments, a **Redact** tool can be used to black out sections that contain the watermark.

Cropping or redacting is most handy for certificates, forms, or scanned PDFs, where the watermark does not overlap any text. However, they won’t work if your watermark is large or appears underneath your PDF’s content.

### #5. Is removing a PDF watermark legal?

Removing a PDF watermark **is legal when you own the content, have explicit permission, or the watermark is your own**. ### #6. Is it possible to edit a watermarked PDF?

Yes, **it’s usually possible to edit a watermarked PDF if it isn’t password-protected or restricted**; in such cases, editors or PDF tools allow you to change text and images, though the visible watermark will stay in your file unless you remove it. Still, if the watermark is flattened or the file is secured, editing becomes much harder and may require OCR or rebuilding pages.
