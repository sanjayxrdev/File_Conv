## What is an Electronic Signature?

An electronic signature, or e-signature, is a **copy of your handwritten signature**. Like your [physical signature](https://pdf.net/blog/what-is-a-wet-signature), it can be used to [sign PDFs](https://pdf.net/sign-pdf) digitally, including contracts, agreements, and other documents.

That said, the appearance of electronic signatures can vary depending on the method you use to create them. For instance, signing a piece of paper and scanning it will likely create the most accurate copy of your signature compared with an online tool.

### Electronic vs. Digital Signature

While the terms electronic and digital are often used interchangeably to refer to an online signature, there is actually a distinct difference between electronic and digital signatures.

Electronic signatures are essentially a **copy of your physical signature **and can be in the form of an image or a scan. Because of this, it can sometimes be difficult to authenticate e-signatures since anyone can upload someone else’s signature. 

However, electronic signatures are also very easy to add; practically any text and PDF editor gives you the option to include your signature. That makes it convenient to use, despite the potential security risks.

[Digital signatures](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/news/understanding-digital-signatures), on the other hand, are **encrypted with a private key**, ensuring their authenticity. When they are applied, only the signer and the receiver can view the document, and it loses its validity if any changes are recorded after signing.

As a result, digital signatures are considered highly secure and reliable, but they require [specialized software](https://pdf.net/blog/best-way-to-sign-documents-online) to apply.

## Are Electronic Signatures Legally Binding?

Electronic signatures **can be legally binding under the [Electronic Signatures in Global and National Commerce Act](https://www.fdic.gov/consumer-compliance-examination-manual) **(ESIGN Act). This means that any online file bearing your electronic signature will be treated the same as a document that you physically signed.

However, to be legally binding, the e-signature process must have the following:

- Intent to sign
- Consent to sign
- Signature attribution
- Signature protection
- Copy sent to the signer
- Record retention
- Opt-out clause

So, if you want to sign a PDF legally with an e-signature, it’s crucial to follow these regulations; otherwise, the document may not hold up in court.

## 3 Common Issues When Making Electronic Signatures and How to Fix Them

Common issues when making electronic signatures include the following:

- **Blurry signature**. If your electronic signature turns out blurry, you could increase image resolution, change the [DPI](https://www.lenovo.com/us/en/glossary/whatis-is-dpi/) in settings (ideally to 72 or 96), and adjust scaling to 100%. You can also use online tools to refine the edges and sharpen the image.
- **Non-transparent background**. When you scan your signature using your phone, the white background you’ve written on will be visible. This problem is pretty easy to fix, though; use a photo editor like Canva to remove the background and keep only the signature. Then, make sure to save the file in [PNG format](https://pdf.net/pdf-to-png), as it handles transparency much better than JPEG.
- **Issues with adding the signature to the document**. Difficulties with adding your signature may arise from many reasons; for instance, your [PDF reader](https://pdf.net/blog/best-pdf-readers) may be outdated, or the document may have some restrictions. That’s why it’s important to always check your program version and permissions, as well as to use the correct tools for signing.
