### #3. Create a Single-Page PDF from Scratch

Creating a single-page PDF from scratch **gives you complete control** over the content and layout. You should begin with a blank document in your preferred platform and **set the page size first**, so you know exactly what you are working with.

Then, it is important to **structure your content carefully** and ensure everything fits on one page without looking cramped. Setting consistent spacing between paragraphs and headings and [using white space to separate ideas](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/346893714_Influence_of_white_space_on_text_scanning) can make your content easier to scan.

Once you are happy with your document, **export it as a PDF. **Here is how you can do that for Word, Excel, and PowerPoint:

#### Word

![how to save one page of a pdf - word](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/i16te7yp/production/b5ccbae0176b6f8f9919a2e4d6100c548cf3a43d-691x296.png?fit=max&auto=format)

1. Select _File_ from the top toolbar.
2. Click _Save_ _as…_ in the dropdown.
3. _Choose PDF_ under _Export_ _formats_ in the dropdown menu.
4. Pick _Best for electronic distribution_… or _Best for printing_.
5. Press _Export_.

#### Excel

![how to save one page of a pdf - excel](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/i16te7yp/production/41a00a3db2cd222511bbd26ffc7847f94ce19432-686x280.png?fit=max&auto=format)

1. Click _File_ from the toolbar at the top of the page.
2. Press _Save as…_ from the dropdown options.
3. Choose _PDF_ as the file format.
4. Tick _Workbook_ or _Sheet_.
5. Select _Export_.

#### PowerPoint

![how to save one page of a pdf - powerpoint](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/i16te7yp/production/60590a0f9cb6d36e15bb82ddadb6890a837277ca-788x235.png?fit=max&auto=format)

1. Choose _File_ in the toolbar.
2. Pick _Save as…_ from the options that appear.
3. Change the file format from PPT to PDF.
4. Click _Export_.

## 4 Tips for Optimizing Your Single-Page PDF

After getting your single-page PDF, you can take a few extra steps to optimize it by:

- **Choosing fonts carefully. **The fonts you choose can directly [affect how easy it is for users to read](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339278256_The_Effects_of_Font_Type_and_Spacing_of_Text_for_Online_Readability_and_Performance) your content. If they are currently harsh on the eyes, you should test out options on both large and small screens. [Change to a font](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-change-font-in-pdf) that allows users to go through your PDF without zooming in and out.
- **Compressing images. **You should compress images before inserting them into your PDF to avoid enlarging the overall file size. Larger files take longer to upload, download, and load, frustrating users and potentially impacting search engine rankings if you [embed PDFs on websites](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-embed-a-pdf-in-a-website).
- **Previewing your PDF on different devices. **Text that looks well-spaced on a large screen may appear cramped on a phone because of the [non-responsive nature of PDFs](https://webstrategy.med.wisc.edu/2022/08/17/usability-and-accessibility-issues-with-pdfs/). Reviewing it across devices helps ensure formatting stays consistent and the document remains easy to read for all users.
- **Checking the final file size before sending or sharing.** If it is too large, use our [_Compress PDF_](https://pdf.net/compress-pdf) tool to make it more manageable. You won’t have to worry about attachment limits, slow uploads, or users struggling to open the PDF on their devices after compression.
