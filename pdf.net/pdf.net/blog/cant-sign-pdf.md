You can't sign a PDF when **the file has signing restrictions, opens in the wrong viewer, or has been flattened, locked, or corrupted,** among other reasons. Most of these problems have a fast fix, be it unlocking the file, switching tools, or adding your signature manually.

PDF signing errors are rather common, particularly with forms, contracts, and scanned documents that pass through several hands before they reach you. So, if you cannot sign a PDF document you need at the moment, you're not alone, and the cause is almost always one of a small handful of issues that can be easily solved.

Today, we walk you through the top seven reasons this happens, how to tell which one applies to you, and the fastest ways to sign a PDF online once you know what's blocking you.

## Key Takeaways

- Most PDF signing problems are related to file restrictions, the wrong viewer, or a flattened or locked document.
- You can check a document's properties or security settings to see whether signing has been disabled.
- Browser-based PDF viewers often can't handle signature fields, but a dedicated PDF editor usually can.
- If your signature disappears after saving, you're likely using a viewer that doesn't support interactive fields.
- An online editor can add a signature to almost any PDF, including flattened, scanned, or locked files.

## Why You Can't Sign a PDF: 7 Common Causes and Fixes

Here are the seven most common culprits if you can’t sign a PDF form or any other document and how to fix each one:

### #1. The PDF Has Signing Restrictions

![Why You Can't Sign a PDF - Signing Restrictions](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/i16te7yp/production/4bd937748bc1ce114950b4b2c2de3a6f940d35d6-1200x896.jpg?fit=max&auto=format)

To begin with, some PDFs are **locked down by whoever created them**, often to prevent unauthorized changes to contracts, legal forms, or financial documents. These signing restrictions can block form filling, commenting, or adding a signature field entirely, even if the file itself isn't password-protected. This is particularly common with PDF forms shared within a company or legal team.

**Fix**: Open the document's properties (in Adobe Acrobat, go to **File **> **Properties **> **Security**) and look for permissions tied to filling forms or signing. If signing is restricted and you're not the owner, ask the person who created the file for a copy with editing enabled. If, however, you already have the password, you can remove the restrictions yourself before signing.

### #2. You're Opening the PDF in a Browser

Browser-based PDF viewers, like the ones built into Chrome, Firefox, and Edge, are built for reading documents, but they often don’t really allow signing. When you open a PDF straight from an email or website, your browser often loads its own limited viewer instead of a full-featured program, so signature fields and form fields may not respond to clicks at all.

**Fix**: Download the PDF to your device first, then open it in a dedicated [PDF reader](https://pdf.net/blog/best-pdf-readers) or online editor. A browser-based editor built specifically for PDFs supports interactive signature fields without the limitations of your browser's built-in viewer.

### #3. The Document Is Flattened

A flattened PDF **turns every interactive element, including signature fields, into a single flat image layer**. This usually happens after a document is printed to PDF, scanned, or exported from certain design programs. Once flattened, there's nothing left for your reader to recognize as a signable field, even if the layout still looks like a form.

**Fix**: The most reliable fix here would be to add your signature as an image or [drawn element](https://pdf.net/blog/how-do-i-draw-on-pdf) directly on top of the flattened layer. An online editor can [make a PDF signable](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-make-a-pdf-signable) again by letting you drop your signature exactly where it needs to go, without any interactive fields required.

### #4. The PDF Is Read-Only

If your PDF opens in read-only mode, **you won't be able to click signature fields, type in form boxes, or change anything at all**. This usually happens when a file is marked read-only at the operating system level, downloaded incorrectly, or protected by the owner to prevent edits.

**Fix**: Check the file's properties on your computer (right-click > **Properties **on Windows, or **Get Info** on Mac) and make sure read-only isn't checked. If the document itself is protected instead, you may need the owner's password to unlock it before you can sign. Downloading a fresh copy from the original source often resolves accidental read-only settings, too.

### #5. Your Signature Tool Isn't Compatible

Not every e-signature tool works with every PDF. Some signature apps rely on proprietary formats or plug-ins that only function within their own [ecosystem](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5294724), so a signature added in one program may not display correctly, or at all, once the file is opened elsewhere.

**Fix**: Standard-compliant tools save signatures directly into the PDF's file structure, so they display properly no matter what program opens the document next. If your current tool keeps failing, you should review alternatives in order to find one that fits your workflow without compatibility headaches.

### #6. The PDF Is Corrupted

![The PDF Is Corrupted](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/i16te7yp/production/c1d3d89d63aee0c76599401283ca213de073c077-1200x896.jpg?fit=max&auto=format)

A corrupted PDF **can break every feature**, including signing, even if the file appears to open normally at first glance. Corruption usually results from an interrupted download, an incomplete file transfer, or a damaged source document.

**Fix**: Try re-downloading the file from its original source, as a fresh copy often solves the problem immediately. If that's not an option, print the PDF to a new file (**Print** > **Save as PDF**, or Microsoft Print to PDF) to generate a clean version. Keep in mind this flattens the document, so you'll need to [add your electronic signature](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-make-an-electronic-signature) as an image instead of typing into a field.

### #7. You're Missing a Digital Signature Certificate

Some PDFs, especially official **government forms, legal contracts, or documents requiring extra verification**, need a [digital signature rather than a simple electronic one](https://pdf.net/blog/difference-between-electronic-and-digital-signature). It uses an encrypted certificate tied to your identity, built on standards maintained by [NIST](https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/digital-signatures), and it won't work if that certificate isn't installed on your device.

**Fix**: If a digital signature’s not working, check whether the document truly requires a certificate-based signature or whether a standard electronic one is enough. For most everyday documents, like [NDAs](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-sign-an-nda), invoices, and internal forms, an electronic signature is legally accepted and far easier to add than a certificate-based digital one.

## How to Tell Whether a PDF Supports Signing

You can tell whether a PDF supports signing by checking for **visible signature fields, a pen **or **sign icon in the toolbar**, and **whether the cursor changes when you hover over the form area**. If nothing changes and no fields appear, the document likely won't let you sign without extra steps.

Most modern PDF programs, including online editors, highlight fillable fields as soon as you open a document. Hover your cursor over the area where a signature belongs; if it turns into a text cursor or shows a pen icon, the field is [interactive](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-make-pdfs-interactive).

You can also check the toolbar for a dedicated **Sign **or **Fill & Sign** option. If the button is grayed out or missing entirely, the PDF may have restrictions, or it could be flattened rather than a true fillable form.

Finally, **look at the file itself**. Scanned documents, faxed pages, and old exported PDFs are usually flat images and won't have interactive fields, similar to what happens with [scanned PDFs](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-edit-scanned-pdfs) that need extra work before they're editable.

## 7 Practical Ways to Sign a PDF Online

Once you know your PDF is signable, you have several ways to actually add your signature. Each method works a little differently depending on your device, the tools you already use, and whether you need someone else's signature, too.

Let’s see what you can do:

- **Use an online PDF editor**. Upload a PDF, draw or type your signature, and download the signed file, all from your browser.
- **Use your operating system's built-in tool**. Mac users can sign directly in Preview, [the system’s PDF editor](https://pdf.net/blog/best-pdf-editor-for-mac), while Windows users can rely on Edge's built-in PDF tools.
- **Insert a scanned or photographed signature**. Sign a blank piece of paper, scan or photograph it, then [insert the image](https://pdf.net/add-image-to-pdf) into your document.
- **Request a signature from someone else**. If you need another person's signature, you can [send a signature request](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-request-a-signature-on-a-pdf) instead of asking them to print and scan the document.
- **Sign from your phone**. Most mobile PDF apps let you sign a PDF (on [iPhone](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-sign-pdf-on-iphone) or Android) using your finger or a stylus.
- **Reuse a saved signature file**. Save a transparent image of your signature once, then drop it into future documents without redrawing it every time.

## 4 Best Practices to Avoid PDF Signing Problems

Here are a few simple rules you should follow if you want to avoid running into PDF signing problems in the first place:

### #1. Always Download the File First

If you open a PDF directly from a browser tab or email preview, you increase the chance that signature fields won't respond. Therefore, it’s best to **download the file to your device before signing it**, and then open it in a PDF tool.

### #2. Keep Your PDF Software Updated

Outdated PDF readers and browser plug-ins are a common cause of a PDF signature not working correctly. Luckily, **regular updates** typically patch compatibility issues and add support for newer signature formats.

### #3. Verify Permissions Before You Send a Document

If you're creating a form for others to sign, **double-check that filling and signing permissions are enabled before you share it**. This keeps recipients from running into the same restrictions you might have faced.

### #4. Use a Secure, Trusted Signing Tool

Not all signing tools protect your data equally, so you should choose a platform that encrypts files in transit, limits unnecessary storage, and helps preserve [document integrity](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/9781119987390.ch3) after the PDF is signed. Additionally, you must confirm that [electronic signatures are safe](https://pdf.net/blog/are-electronic-signatures-safe) with that provider before sending sensitive documents.

## Can't Sign PDF FAQs

### #1. Can you sign a PDF document without Adobe?

Yes, **you can sign a PDF document without [Adobe](https://pdf.net/blog/is-adobe-acrobat-worth-it)**. Browser-based tools let you add a signature directly to any PDF without installing Acrobat or any other desktop software, so you can complete and download signed documents from any device with internet access.

### #2. Can I sign a locked PDF?

You can sign a locked PDF **once the restrictions blocking form fields and signatures are removed**. If you have the password, [unlocking the file](https://pdf.net/unlock-pdf) restores full signing access, but without it, you'll need to contact the owner and request an unrestricted copy or their permission to sign.

### #3. Why does my signature disappear after saving?

Your signature disappears after saving because **you likely used a viewer that doesn't save interactive elements, or the file flattened during export**. The solution is to save with a full-featured PDF editor instead of a browser preview to keep the signature layer intact.

### #4. Why can't I sign a PDF on my phone?

You can't sign a PDF on your phone **if your device's default viewer doesn't support signature fields**, which is rather common with basic mobile browsers. Use a PDF app built for mobile signing instead, or open the file in a browser-based editor that works on both iOS and [Android](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-sign-documents-on-android).
