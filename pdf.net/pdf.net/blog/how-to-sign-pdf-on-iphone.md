## 4 Valuable Tips for Signing PDFs on iPhones

Keep these four tips in mind when signing PDFs on your iPhone:

### #1. Ensure Your Signature Is Clear and Accurate

For an electronic signature to be [legally recognized](https://pdf.net/blog/is-electronic-signature-legal), it must **clearly represent your identity and show your intent to sign. **That said, a messy scribble or partially cut-off signature can raise questions.

To make sure your signature on an iPhone is clear and accurate, you need to **sign slowly and deliberately;** use your finger or a stylus to write at a moderate pace. If the signature looks distorted, it is best to undo it and try again. After inserting your signature, you can drag the corners to resize it proportionally.

### #2. Use a Stylus for More Precision

It is easy to make shaky strokes when signing with your finger; a stylus can** make your signature look more natural and professional.**

Before you begin, ensure the stylus is **designed to support** iPhones. A few common options are Fiftythree Digital Stylus, Adonit Pixel, and MoKo Universal Active Stylus Pen. If your stylus has a battery, **confirm it is charged** so it won’t lag or cause your strokes to break.

Next, find a **stable surface to rest your palm** on, as this helps you maintain consistent pressure to make steadier strokes. If there is a signature box on the PDF, don’t try to squeeze your signature into that tiny space. Instead, you should **sign freely outside, then resize and reposition it **to fit the box.

### #3. Keep a Saved Digital Signature for Repeated Use

Re-signing your name every time you open a PDF is tedious and can lead to inconsistencies. These not only **make your documents appear unprofessional** but also **raise doubts. **It is much better to take the time to create and save a single master signature to reuse. Both _Markup_ and pdf.net allow you to do this.

However, you must **be more careful about your iPhone’s security**. Since your signature is stored and available for you to apply to PDFs whenever you want, anyone who can access your iPhone can do so without your permission.

To reduce this risk, you should **enable Face ID or Touch ID**. Studies comparing facial and fingerprint recognition with traditional passwords and PINs have found that [biometric methods generally provide stronger security](https://www.ijert.org/are-biometrics-truly-better?) than short numeric passcodes, particularly 4-digit ones.

Even if you choose to use passcodes, **go with one that’s longer than 4 digits**. [Research comparing 4- and 6-digit passcodes](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04959?) in shoulder surfing attacks, for example, found that 6-digit ones were much harder for attackers to guess from observations.

You should also **avoid saving your signature on shared devices**, and be cautious about leaving your phone unattended. Additionally, you can consider setting your phone to lock automatically after a short period of inactivity for an extra layer of security.

### #4. Check the Signed Document Before Sending

Don’t send your PDF right after applying your signature, but take a couple of seconds to review that everything is good to go.

First, zoom in to check that your signature is **not hovering above the signature line or overlapping surrounding text. **Your signature should **look proportional to the rest of the document. **If it appears oversized or stretched, you can adjust it by dragging the corners.

Then, you need to **verify that you have signed all the fields** **required** in your PDF. It is common to miss additional signature boxes on later pages, so go through each page carefully.

## Troubleshooting Tips for Signing PDFs on iPhones

If you are having trouble signing a PDF on your iPhone, don’t panic; most issues can be addressed easily.

- **The _Markup_ option is missing**. If you are previewing the PDF from a browser or a [link](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-create-a-link-to-a-pdf), editing tools may not appear. So, [save the PDF](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-save-as-a-pdf) to the _Files_ app. When you open it, you should be able to find the pen icon and access _Markup_.
- **The signature is distorted after resizing.** This tends to happen when you drag from one side. You need to use the corner handles to stretch all sides evenly if you want to maintain the correct proportions.
- **The stylus is not responding**. Is it fully charged and connected? If you can’t get your device working, check the battery levels and settings, then try to reconnect it.
- **Document won’t allow editing**. The PDF may be locked or flattened. In this case, you can try opening it in a dedicated reader or request an editable version from the sender.
