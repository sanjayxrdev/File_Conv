## Resizing a PDF: File Size vs. Page Dimensions

When we talk about resizing a PDF, that can mean two things: **reducing the file size through compression or changing page dimensions through scaling**. These two have different purposes and outcomes, which we would define as follows:

- **PDF compression** is essentially reducing the file size to make it smaller and easier to share. However, the document’s appearance doesn’t change on screen or in print, unless the compression is high enough to blur text and images.
- **PDF dimension changes** physically alter the size of the pages to achieve a certain goal; for instance, when the PDF might have to be scaled up or down to fit printing requirements. In this case, the changes are obvious and don’t affect just the file size.

Now, here’s a brief overview of the differences between the two:

|  | PDF compression | PDF dimension changes (scaling) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Purpose** | Decreasing the file size (MB) | Changing the page size |
| **Appearance** | Stays the same | Changes with scaling or cropping |
| **Quality** | High compression may cause blurring | Potential blurring, difficulty reading the text |
| **Used for** | Storage saving, easier sharing, or uploading to websites with file size limits | Printing, fitting specific paper sizes or requirements |

It’s worth noting that, in this article, **we will focus on PDF compression**, not scaling. With that in mind, keep reading to find out how to change the size of your file with ease.

## Why and When Do You Need to Resize PDF Documents?

You need to resize PDF documents because it eases the sharing, uploading, and loading process when using these files for business, school projects, and other occasions.

Let’s see the most common situations when you need to resize PDFs:

- **[Email attachments](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-email-large-pdf-file) and uploads**. Most email platforms have a **25 MB limit **for file attachments. Other platforms (e.g., social media) also have similar limits. Therefore, resizing PDF files can help you send your documents via email hassle-free and share them easily on various platforms.
- **Using PDF optimizers for faster loading and sharing**. You can [optimize PDFs for web](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-optimize-pdf-for-web) pages, which lowers their size and allows [web crawlers](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/bots/what-is-a-web-crawler/) to recognize the file more quickly. This also reduces the chances of crossing the size limit or getting stuck during a transfer due to oversized files.
- **Adapting files for business and government portals**. Business and government portals may require you to upload your documents in the PDF file format for readability and safety. In such cases, it’s easier to upload low-size PDFs.

However, as we’ve established, resizing isn’t just changing the file size; there are situations when you may need to change the dimensions of the pages. Here are some examples:

- **Meeting printing requirements**. Some documents may need to be printed in a specific way (e.g., in an A4 format), in which case you might need to adjust the page size. By doing so, you ensure that all content fits, and there are no excessively large margins.
- **Binding purposes**. If you’re planning to bind your printed document, the margins typically need to be larger to accommodate this process. In this case, resizing will help you achieve the best text-to-blank-space ratio.
- **Mobile viewing**. Resizing pages can also help you read the content better on small screens like smartphones and tablets. Consider this option when you’re struggling to read a document.

## 3 Best Practices for Resizing PDFs

Compression software can be tricky, especially low-quality and free PDF resizing tools. Specifically, unstable compression may corrupt or poorly resize your files, leaving you with an unfavorable outcome.

Let’s examine the best tips for effortless PDF resizing:

### #1. Maintain a Balance Between File Size and Quality

Both file size and quality are important, so you shouldn’t sacrifice one for the sake of the other. In other words, documents that are too large are not practical for sharing and using, while low-quality files can be unreadable and, therefore, useless.

Finding a balance between the two can be the best solution, as it allows for smaller and higher-quality files. To do so, you can use tools such as pdf.net, which offers a **balanced compression level**.

### #2. Choose the Right Compression Settings

Another tip on how to resize PDFs is to choose your compression type based on how much you need to decrease the file size. Once you know the resizing purpose (e.g., sending a PDF via email), you can choose between** high, medium, and low** compression.

If you aim for the smallest size, you can use lossy (high) compression, which will affect the file quality. If not, you can use [lossless (low) compression](https://www.futurelearn.com/info/courses/introduction-to-digital-media/0/steps/428809) and reduce the file size a little while keeping its original quality. Medium (balanced) compression can lower the size of your document decently and minimally affect its quality, but it may not be adequate for huge files.

### #3. Use Batch Processing for Multiple PDFs

Resizing numerous files separately can be tedious and time-consuming. On the other hand, using online PDF resizers with batch processing saves time and simplifies the process.

You can streamline this process by uploading multiple PDFs from your drive to a designated platform, selecting the compression level for all documents at once or for each one separately, and clicking the resize or compress button.

## 3 Common Issues With Resizing PDFs and Troubleshooting Tips

Resizing PDFs without losing quality is not always easy, as you may not get the best compression result, and the process may even damage your files.

Let’s learn more about the most common problems and how to troubleshoot them:

### #1. Loss of Quality After Resizing

Loss of quality is the most common compression problem because certain compression algorithms (e.g., lossy compression) remove some PDF elements to reduce size.

There are several ways to maintain and restore quality before or after compression, with the best ones being:

- **Using lossless compression**. Low** **or** **lossless compression may not shrink your files too much but will prevent quality loss. To preserve quality, you can use the JBIG2 compression algorithm for text and the PNG algorithm for images. If your first attempt resulted in lossy compression, you can re-compress it with these settings if you have the original file.
- **Re-scanning PDFs for better quality**. You can regain PDF quality (at least for the most part) using the online scanning software. To do so, scan your document at a high dots-per-inch ratio ([DPI](https://www.solopress.com/blog/art-design/what-is-dpi/)) and set the** maximum scan resolution **(300 or higher) for the best results.

### #2. Corrupted PDF After Resizing

Corrupted files are another common compression problem, usually related to low-quality software. If you end up getting one, you can recover it by:

- **Using PDF repair software**. Online PDF repair software finds and recovers inaccessible data in damaged PDFs. The success rate varies according to the level of corruption, but it is generally high.
- **Restore the previous version or use a backup file**. Some platforms save original documents on the cloud as customers use their tools, meaning you can **retrieve the original** **file** if the new document is damaged. In this case, it is important not to exit the browser tab where your document is open.
- **Convert to another format and do back-conversion**. You can convert the faulty PDF file to another format (e.g., turn [PDF into Word](https://pdf.net/pdf-to-word)) and then back to PDF to repair it. This method does not have a high success rate, but it is worth trying.
- **Try a different reader**. Sometimes, only specific readers can open damaged PDFs. Switching between different options may help you find one that will let you open broken files and access some or all their parts.

### #3. File Too Large Even After Compression

Even if you use the best PDF compressor, huge PDF documents may only become less large and still be beyond the size limit for some platforms. You can tackle this issue in a few simple ways:

- **Resize images and limit embedded fonts**. After resizing, you can manually compress PDF images. As these typically take up a lot of space, this will preserve their quality while lowering the size of your file.
- **Separate your PDF into smaller ones**. If you can [split your document](https://pdf.net/split-pdf) into multiple ones logically, this may be a good way to transfer, upload, and **share multiple **documents separately. The receiver can later merge them into one if needed.
- **Remove unnecessary elements and flatten layers**. If your PDF contains comments, [annotations](https://pdf.net/annotate-pdf), bookmarks, and other unnecessary elements, you can remove them to make it smaller. You can also flatten your PDF to make all layers uniform, which can significantly reduce your file size.
