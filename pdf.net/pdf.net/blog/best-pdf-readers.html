What to Look for in a PDF Reader

What you should look for in a PDF reader depends on how you use PDFs day-to-day. Someone who mostly reads manuals cares about load speed and navigation, while someone handling contracts needs bookmarks, search, and a reliable way to add a signature. Here are some of the key features to look for:

  • Easy way to upload documents
  • Capacity to process lengthy documents without lag
  • Simple navigation options, like one-click buttons or thumbnail pages on a sidebar
  • Option to create bookmarks
  • Option to zoom in and zoom out
  • Option to switch between light and dark modes
  • Responsive design on mobile devices
  • Read-aloud functionality for accessibility
  • Option to search the PDF
  • No watermarks

#1. SumatraPDF

This is a lightweight PDF reader for Windows users, packed with fast, free features. Aside from PDF, you can use it to open EPUB, mobi, cbz, cbr, DjVu, XPS, and CHM files.

Here's what you get:

  • Keyboard shortcuts for easy navigation
  • Saves your reading progress, with frequently and recently read documents displayed on the home screen
  • Different viewing modes
  • Advanced configuration options, like default zoom level and keyboard shortcut remapping

Now, let’s take a quick look at SumatraPDF advantages and disadvantages:

Pros

Cons

  • Fastest of every tool we tested — 0.66 seconds to launch and 0.82 seconds to open a 50MB file
  • Cannot be used on Mac devices
  • User-friendly interface
  • No annotation or editing tools at all in our testing. No highlighting, comments, drawing, sticky notes, or signatures
  • Option to customize and configure your reading experience

    SumatraPDF works best for people who value speed and simplicity over editing power: students reading lecture notes, researchers reviewing academic papers, or employees skimming manuals and reports.

    It topped our testing for launch speed and interface clarity, but it's worth knowing upfront that it has zero annotation or editing tools, so it's a poor fit if you need to mark up or sign PDFs.

                        4 Benefits of Using PDF Readers

                        More teams now work from outside a traditional office; namely, 22.6% of U.S. workers teleworked in March 2026, per the Bureau of Labor Statistics. PDF readers are important for remote teams because they keep documents consistent no matter who or where opens them.

                        Below are the top four benefits of using PDF readers:

                        1. More accurate. Unlike web browsers and word processors that often reflow text, PDF readers preserve the exact layout to make documents easier to read and understand. This makes them critical for exams, manuals, legal documents, and official forms.
                        2. Better built-in features. Image viewers and browser previews usually only allow scrolling and zooming. PDF readers offer more — thumbnails, bookmarks, and reading modes that help you move through long documents efficiently.
                        3. Faster processing with complex files. PDF readers are designed to handle large documents with images, vectors, and embedded fonts. They don't encounter lagging, crashing, or rendering problems that are common with Word processors and browsers.
                        4. Reliable form handling. One of the most common uses of a PDF is completing forms. With PDF readers, you can properly interact with dropdowns, checkboxes, and signature lines. Word processors often break form functionality or fail to save entered data.

                        Best PDF Readers FAQs

                        #1. Is there a better PDF reader than Chrome?

                        pdf.net is a better PDF reader than Chrome if you want to open and edit documents in a single browser tab. For example, you can review an invoice and sign it, or fill out a PDF form before sending it off.

                        Yes, you can use a PDF reader online without downloading software through browser-based options like pdf.net and Smallpdf. These save time since there is nothing to install, though you will need a reliable internet connection.

                        #3. Which PDF reader is best for Windows?

                        PDF-XChange and SumatraPDF are built specifically for Windows. pdf.net also works on Windows because it is fully browser-based and is not tied to one operating system.

                        #4. Are there PDF readers for Mac that are free?

                        Yes, there are free PDF readers for Mac. pdf.net is one of the top options since it is browser-based — no download required — and it scored 5/5 for navigation ease in our testing. You will not need to pay unless you want to edit your document.

                        #5. Can I annotate and edit PDFs with a PDF reader?

                        Yes, most PDF readers let you annotate and edit PDFs. SumatraPDF is the one exception on this list: in our tests, it was the only tool with zero annotation tools of any kind — no highlighting, comments, drawing, sticky notes, or signatures.
