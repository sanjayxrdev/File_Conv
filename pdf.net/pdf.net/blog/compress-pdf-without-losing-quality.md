## What Is PDF Compression?

PDF compression is the process of **reducing the size** of this type of file. The best way to do so is to use online or offline software designed to identify the document components and then shrink them or remove the ones that are excessive or doubled.

There are several types of compression, all of which are based on compression levels—**low, default, or high**.

1. **Low compression** is the best way to reduce PDF size without quality loss. It only makes minor changes to your document and mildly reduces the size of your file.
2. **Default compression** is when an online tool **finds the new optimal size** for your document based on its original size and components. This type of compression may not shrink your file too much but may also not lower its quality.
3. **High compression** can **dramatically reduce the size** of your file, but it may also significantly diminish the quality of your document.

In general, compressing your files allows you to open, use, and process them more easily. Besides that, by doing so, you can **upload and share** low-sized PDFs faster and error-free.

In the past year alone, pdf.net users have **freed up over 2.5 TB of storage** this way, reducing file sizes by an average of 77%. This is real proof that compression can make a real difference without compromising quality.

## How PDF Compression Affects the Quality of Files

PDF compression affects the quality of files in two ways—it may lower readability or decrease document quality.

Generally speaking, it’s essential to know about the two types of compression—**high-quality PDF compression** (or [lossless](https://imagify.io/blog/lossless-vs-lossy-image-compression/) PDF compression) and **lossy PDF compression**, as choosing between them can directly determine the post-compression file status.

Specifically, using lossy compression can lower the readability and/or quality of your documents. This is because this compression method removes components from your documents or shrinks them so that they lose some elements. By choosing a ‘high or strong’ compression type, which will significantly reduce your file size, you are also choosing lossy compression.

On the other hand, you can use lossless PDF compression instead; it removes only unnecessary or duplicate files or compresses files while **restoring the elements whose quality was lowered**. This way, you will have small-sized, high-quality documents.

That said, **maintaining the quality of PDFs after lossless compression** will enhance your working efficiency by keeping your document readable, accessible, and easy to edit or use.

## 5 Tips for Improving PDF Quality Before and After Compression

Compression may bring various challenges, most of which relate to the quality of PDF files. Still, there are ways to prevent these issues and improve the process.

Let’s check out some useful** tips on improving PDF quality** before and after compression:

### #1. Choose the Right PDF Tool

We recommend using **dedicated compression software** to avoid receiving error messages during compression. Unreliable tools may damage or [corrupt](https://in.indeed.com/career-advice/career-development/corrupted-file) your files, rendering them useless if you don’t create a backup.

Moreover, low-quality PDF compressors may shrink your file but lower its quality and readability at the same time. Therefore, to avoid trouble, use high-quality software** such as pdf.net** for lossless compression.

### #2. Split Large Files Before Compression

Some online tools may not allow you to upload files that exceed a few GBs in size. To bypass this, you can [**split your PDFs into multiple files**](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-split-pdf-into-multiple-files). If you use reliable software for this, you will preserve the document quality. Moreover, you will be able to upload smaller files, compress them, and [merge them](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-merge-pdfs) again to get the same document as before, only with a smaller size.

### #3. Adjust Image Resolution and Limit Embedded Fonts

You can **lower the image resolution** even before conversion. If you do this manually and use distinct tools, there is a higher possibility that the image quality will not be reduced. You can then replace large-sized images with low-sized ones, which will automatically reduce the size of your entire PDF file.

Similarly, embedded fonts in a PDF document can increase its size; however, if you **remove unique fonts** and keep only the standard ones, the size of your file will decrease.

### #4. Delete Unnecessary Elements and Run Tests

You can manually reduce the size of your document before compression by removing all extra elements from your document. Specifically, you should target all **comments, annotations, [layers](https://www.picmonkey.com/blog/use-layers-in-your-designs-and-photo-editing), or bookmarks** you find insignificant.

In addition, before you compress the desired file, you can try to compress a random PDF file to see how a certain tool will perform. This is a useful precaution that can save your document from getting damaged.

### #5. Do Post-Editing

Even if you use high-quality compression tools, your file's post-compression quality may still be slightly lower than its original one. If you have already followed pre-compression tips to keep its important characteristics, you can **rescan your PDF** to enhance its post-compression quality.

In other words, you should rescan your document at a higher **dots per inch ratio** ([DPI](https://www.gflesch.com/blog/standard-dpi-for-printed-work)) by using a scanner and disabling automatic configuration and size reduction in the settings menu. Plus, we advise you to use the highest possible resolution that a scanner offers if you choose a resolution exceeding 300.
