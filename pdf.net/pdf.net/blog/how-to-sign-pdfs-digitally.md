## 3 Crucial Mistakes to Avoid When Signing PDFs Digitally

Since digital signatures are becoming more common, it’s important to familiarize yourself with the **common mistakes you should avoid when signing PDFs digitally**.

These include:

- **Using suspicious software**. When signing documents that contain private information, you should use secure digital signature tools like pdf.net; otherwise, you may risk leaking sensitive data.
- **Using a different signature**. Your digital signature is legal, so it’s important to use the verified version when signing PDFs digitally, too. Using a different signature may nullify the validity of the document and render any contracts or agreements void.
- **Using an image of the signature**. You can’t just upload an image of your handwritten signature and use it as a digital signature. Digital signatures are encrypted to ensure that they cannot be forged, so simply using an image will make its authenticity questionable.

## How to Sign PDFs Digitally FAQs

### #1. Is a digital signature legally binding?

Yes, a **digital or electronic signature is legally binding **in states covered by federal law as long as it meets the requirements of the ESIGN Act. In states where federal law doesn’t apply, digital signatures may still be legally binding if they meet the [Uniform Electronic Transactions Act](https://law.lis.virginia.gov/vacodepopularnames/uniform-electronic-transactions-act/) (UETA) requirements.

### #2. Can I sign PDFs on mobile devices?

Yes, **you can sign PDFs on mobile devices using pdf.net**. As cloud-based software, you can easily sign PDFs digitally on our tool as long as you have a stable internet connection. Simply open the PDF editor on your mobile browser and add your signature.

### #3. Why is my PDF not allowing a digital signature?

If your PDF does not allow a digital signature, it’s likely because the **file is set to read-only mode**. This is why it’s important to prepare the document before requesting any signatures.
