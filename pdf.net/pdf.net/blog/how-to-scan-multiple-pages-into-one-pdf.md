To scan multiple pages into one PDF, you can use Windows Fax & Scan or Windows Scan on a PC, or Preview and Image Capture on a Mac. On mobile, applications such as Notes, Genius Scan, CamScanner, or Google Drive make the process just as simple.

It is important to know how to use these tools since scanning one page at a time often leaves you with a folder full of separate files that are hard to organize and even harder to share.

A single PDF keeps everything in one place, so it’s quick to send to others, store, and search through later. In this guide, we will cover different ways to scan multiple pages into one PDF on Windows, Mac, iOS, and Android, along with tips on how to make the process even simpler.

## Key Takeaways

- Scanning multiple pages into one PDF **helps with file organization, sharing, and editing or archiving**.
- Windows users can scan multiple pages with Windows Fax and Scan to PDF, the Windows Scan app, or their printer manufacturer's software.
- Mac users can rely on Preview or Image Capture.
- On mobile, Notes on iPhone, and Genius Scan, Google Drive, or CamScanner on Android all let you scan multiple pages into a single PDF
- Most all-in-one printers can scan multiple pages directly to PDF from the control panel, without installing any extra software on your computer.
- Cleaning your scanner glass, using an Automatic Document Feeder, and naming files consistently prevent the most common multi-page scanning problems.

## Why Scan Multiple Pages Into One PDF?

Scanning multiple pages into one PDF has benefits for both personal and professional use.

First, it is **convenient for sharing and emailing**; you do not need to send dozens of documents, and your recipient does not have to manage multiple attachments. With everything bundled neatly into one PDF, there is a **lower risk of losing or overlooking important pages**.

Furthermore, it makes it **easier to organize files**. Combining multiple pages into a single PDF keeps related documents together, making them **simple to store, search, and retrieve**.

This is especially important if you often handle large volumes of documents; for example, if you are a lawyer managing case files, a researcher compiling studies, or a business owner dealing with financial records.

Finally, scanning several pages into one PDF **simplifies editing, annotating, and archiving**. You can use PDF editors to [add highlights](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-highlight-text-in-pdf) and comments or [rearrange pages](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-rearrange-pdf-pages) without having to juggle multiple files. For long-term storage, a single PDF takes up less space. If you want physical copies for peace of mind, printing one document is also much simpler.

### #1. How to Scan Multiple Pages into One Document on Windows 10 or 11

If you are a Windows user, you can scan multiple pages to PDF on Windows 11 or 10 by using **Windows Fax and Scan** and capture every page in a single session instead of saving each one separately. The process works the same way on both OS, and you should follow these steps:

1. Connect your scanner to your PC and open **Windows Fax and Scan**. ![Connect your scanner to your PC and open Windows Fax and Scan](https://cdn.sanity.io/images/i16te7yp/production/343a93c105c4676aba8353414bf60058974a260b-1918x1000.png?fit=max&auto=format)

3. Click **Scan more pages** to keep adding pages to the same document.
4. Once every page is captured, save the file to your PC.

For a simpler interface, you can try scanning multiple pages to PDF with the **Windows Scan app** from the Microsoft Store or **with NAPS2** (Not Another PDF Scanner 2), a free program that lets you adjust scan quality. ## How to Scan to PDF Directly From a Printer or Scanner (No Software)

You can scan to PDF directly from a printer or scanner by **selecting PDF as the output format on the machine's control panel and saving the file to a USB drive or a shared network folder**, all without installing any extra software.

Most all-in-one printers support this because they have their own onboard scanning menu, which works independently of any app on your computer.

To do this, you should:

1. Connect the printer to your network or plug in a USB drive, then load your first page.
2. On the printer's touchscreen, look for a **Scan** option, rather than Copy or Print, and choose **Scan to USB** or **Scan to Network Folder**.
3. Select **PDF** from the file type menu, and if the option is available, turn on **Multi-Page** or **Batch Scan** mode so the printer keeps adding pages to the same file until you tap **Finish** or **Done**.
4. Once you're done, plug the USB drive into your computer or open the shared folder to find your combined PDF.

If your printer saves each page as a separate file instead of combining them, you can always use an online [merge tool](https://pdf.net/merge-pdf) like pdf.net afterward to bring the pages together into one document.

## Troubleshooting Scanning Issues

Scanning issues are usually caused by **incorrect output settings, disorganized batches, or outdated drivers**, and most are quick to fix. Here are the most common problems and how to solve each one.

### #1. Scanner Not Saving as PDF

When your scanner defaults to image formats like JPEG or [TIFF](https://www.loc.gov/preservation/digital/formats/fdd/fdd000022.shtml), check your scan settings and switch the output format to PDF. If your scanner doesn’t support it, you can use pdf.net to [convert images to a PDF](https://pdf.net/jpg-to-pdf) after scanning.

On Windows, if the Windows Scan app only offers JPEG or PNG, select **Microsoft Print to PDF** as the printer when opening the image to save it as a proper PDF.

### #2. Pages Out of Order

This often happens when batch mode is disabled or when the pages were scanned separately. To avoid it, be sure to scan sequentially; if you have done so but realize later on that there are mistakes, you can use pdf.net to [rearrange the pages](https://pdf.net/rearrange-pdf) before exporting the final file.

### #3. File Size Too Large

The file size can become excessively large when there are many pages scanned and combined. A workaround is to lower the scan resolution (e.g., 200 [DPI](https://www.lenovo.com/gb/en/glossary/whatis-is-dpi/) instead of 600 DPI). If that is not possible, you can [compress the PDF](https://pdf.net/blog/compress-pdf-without-losing-quality) using pdf.net afterward.

### #4. Software Crashes or Fails to Recognize Multiple Scans

Outdated scanner drivers are the most common cause of crashes or scans that won't combine into a batch. Update your drivers and restart the application, and scan in smaller batches to avoid overloading the software. Switching to a lightweight application, such as NAPS2, Genius Scan, or CamScanner, can also help if the manufacturer's software keeps freezing.

## 6 Powerful Tips for Efficient Multi-Page Scanning

Here are six game-changing tips for efficient multi-page scanning:

1. **Use an [Automatic Document Feeder (ADF)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/1244002.1244324). **If your scanner has an ADF, take advantage of it. This feature allows you to load a stack of pages that the machine scans automatically, saving you from placing each sheet manually.
2. **Flatten and clean documents. **Remember to remove staples, paper clips, and folds so pages feed smoothly and scans look professional. This will also reduce the chance of paper jams that can interrupt your scanning session.
3. **Scan in batches if needed. **Doing so helps you avoid software crashes. Rest assured that it is a lot more time-consuming to subsequently merge the batches with online PDF merge tools like pdf.net.
4. **Preview before the final save. **Most scanning software shows a preview of each page. Use this to double-check the alignment before you commit to saving, so you do not have to redo entire batches.
5. **Name files consistently. **This will make searching, sorting, and archiving much easier later on. You just need to adopt a simple naming system, like “ProjectA_Report_September2025.pdf.”
6. **Save backups to [cloud storage](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/cloud-storage). **This means uploading your scanned PDFs to services like Google Drive, Dropbox, or OneDrive. In case of hardware failure, you will still have secure access to your documents and can quickly restore them.

## How to Scan Multiple Pages into One PDF FAQs

### #1. How do I scan multiple pages into one PDF without Adobe?

To scan multiple pages into one PDF without Adobe, you can use **Windows Fax & Scan, Preview **on Mac**, Notes** on iPhone, **Google** **Drive**, or free scanning apps like **CamScanner** and **Genius** **Scan**.

### #2. Can I scan multiple pages into one PDF on my phone?

Yes, you can scan multiple pages into one PDF on your phone. Use the **Notes** app if you are on an iPhone or download a scanning app like **CamScanner** or **Genius** **Scan**, which are both available on iOS and Android.

### #3. How to scan double-sided documents into one PDF?

To scan double-sided documents into one PDF, **manually flip the pages, scan the backs, and reorder them using a [PDF editor](https://pdf.net/blog/best-pdf-editors).** If your scanner has duplex mode, it will automatically capture both sides.

### #4. How do I make my scanned PDF searchable?

To make your scanned PDFs searchable, you will need to **use OCR** tools, which you can find online. However, certain scanning apps, like CamScanner, already have OCR built in.

### #5. Can I reduce the file size of a multi-page PDF?

**Yes, you can** **reduce the file size of a multi-page PDF** by using pdf.net’s [compression tool](https://pdf.net/compress-pdf). Reducing resolution, using grayscale instead of color, and removing unnecessary images also help keep the file size smaller.

### #6. Will my pages stay in order when I scan multiple pages to PDF?

Your pages will stay in order when you scan multiple pages to one PDF if you **load pages correctly **or **use an Automatic Document Feeder (ADF)**. In case they do not, you can reorder them in just a few minutes using pdf.net.

### #7. Can I reorder my pages after I scan multiple pages to PDF?

**Yes, you can** **reorder your pages after scanning multiple pages to PDF**. Many applications will offer you this option. If not, you can use pdf.net to drag and drop pages into the correct sequence before exporting as the final PDF.

### #8. Are there differences between free vs. paid PDF scanning solutions?

**Yes, there are** differences between free and paid PDF scanning solutions. The latter often offer advanced features like higher OCR accuracy. However, the tools mentioned above are all free and can still get the job done well.

### #9. Can I use the Windows Scan app to scan multiple pages into one PDF on Windows 11?

Yes, **you can use the Windows Scan app to scan multiple pages into one PDF on Windows 11**. Open Scan from the Start menu, select your scanner, choose PDF as the file type if it's available, and scan each page in sequence. If the app only saves images, print the finished file to Microsoft Print to PDF to combine everything into one document.

### #10. Does Windows Fax and Scan save files as a PDF?

No, **Windows Fax and Scan doesn't save multi-page scans directly as a PDF**; it saves them as TIFF or another image format by default. To get a PDF, open the scanned file and print it using Microsoft Print to PDF, or combine the pages afterward with pdf.net.
