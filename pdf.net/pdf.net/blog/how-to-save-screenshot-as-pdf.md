## Why Save Screenshots as PDFs?

You should save screenshots as PDFs for easier **access, transfer, compatibility, and [annotation](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-annotate-pdf)**.

Let’s take a closer look at the benefits of saving screenshots as PDFs:

- **Preserving quality**. You can secure image quality by [inserting them into PDFs](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-insert-images-into-pdf), which will preserve their** clarity, resolution, brightness, and size**.
- **Easy sharing**. Adding images or screenshots of [SMS text messages](https://krispcall.com/feature/sms-text-messages/) to other PDF content will make it uniform and, therefore, easy to share. Once you pack everything into a PDF file, you can also [resize it](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-resize-pdfs) to facilitate its sharing further.
- **High compatibility.** The PDF format is universal across online clouds and internet platforms. Similarly, if you have a PDF reader, you can always** open, use, and share PDFs** regardless of your device or operating system.
- **Simple annotating**. PDFs allow you to add comments and annotate screenshots within them **quickly and hassle-free**. This is important because adding comments and annotating image-format screenshots can be lengthy and more complicated.

## 3 Common Issues With Saving Screenshots as PDFs and Troubleshooting Tips

Saving screenshots as PDFs can impact the image or document **quality and size**.

Let’s examine these issues and the tips to resolve them:

### #1. PDF Is Blurry

Blurry PDFs can occur because of initial low screenshot quality or after using cheap PDF tools for conversion. To obtain **clear and readable files**, pay attention to the following factors:

- **Screenshot to PDF converter type**.** **Using a reliable online PDF converter such as pdf.net helps you convert and export screenshots into high-quality PDFs.
- **Image resolution and format**. Choose high-resolution screenshots and save them in **lossless formats like PNG **instead of selecting a JPG to PDF conversion. When creating documents from screenshots, a [PNG to PDF](https://pdf.net/png-to-pdf) conversion is often the better choice for preserving image quality.
- **Compression variant**. Using [lossy compression](https://www.elegantthemes.com/blog/wordpress/understanding-lossy-compression-and-when-to-use-it) for PDF documents with screenshots can lower their quality and blur them. This is why we recommend using a medium (balanced) or [lossless compression](https://seahawkmedia.com/design-glossary/lossless-compression-advantages-disadvantages-and-types/).

### #2. Screenshots Deranged or Not Fitting Formatting

After saving screenshots as PDFs, you may notice that some images are too large, small, turned upside down, or deranged. Also, screenshots may be in portrait mode instead of landscape mode.

To prevent and fix these issues, you should use advanced screenshot tools (e.g., Lightshot and Snipping tool) and capture more spatial areas **for large-resolution images**. You can also use resolution-enhancing software to improve the quality of screenshots before integrating them into PDFs and resizing them to make them fit your PDF formatting.

The [rearranging tool](https://pdf.net/rearrange-pdf) can help you reorder deranged screenshots, flip and rotate them, and make your files perfect.

### #3. PDF Is Too Large

Adding images (including screenshots), in general, makes PDF documents larger. This may not be a problem unless you must email or share them on platforms with size limits.

To solve this, you can compress screenshots before converting them to PDF. The lower the original screenshot size is, the lower the final PDF file will be. Additionally, you can use **lossless pdf.net compression** to resize your PDF with screenshots, which is one of the most effective ways to [compress PDFs without losing quality](https://pdf.net/blog/compress-pdf-without-losing-quality).
