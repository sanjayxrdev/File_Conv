How to Create a PDF Portfolio | Easy Step-by-Step Guide

Learn how to create a PDF portfolio in a few simple steps and how to share it with recruiters and clients to boost your applications.

Alex Chen

Technical Content Specialist

Table of contents

To create a PDF portfolio, you should select your best and most representative sample work, design the layout, write impactful project descriptions, and carefully review your first draft. Once this is done, convert your document to PDF to preserve its format and share it easily.

In this article, we will show you exactly how to make a portfolio in a PDF format, which elements to include, and why a polished portfolio may be important for your career. Furthermore, we will teach you how to share it via email or other digital platforms.

  • You should make a PDF portfolio to apply for a job in a creative field, deliver a successful client pitch, or secure your spot in an academic program.

When Do You Need to Create a Portfolio?

You need to create a portfolio when you want to demonstrate your skills through tangible results, land a specific role, or garner support for a particular project.

Here are a few situations where a portfolio can be useful:

  • Applying for a job in a creative field. Designers, photographers, writers, and artists most commonly create portfolios to showcase their style and technique. This is an excellent strategy, as employers can review the samples and immediately decide whether the applicant is the right fit.
  • Making client pitches. Freelancers in various fields reach out to clients directly (or respond to their ads) and offer their services. Though a portfolio isn’t always necessary for this, it can strengthen the pitch significantly, since the client can see the evidence of expertise and experience.
  • Applying for academic programs. Some educational institutions require submitting a portfolio with an application. This is especially common with art programs, architecture, creative writing, and performance arts.

PDF Portfolio vs. Online Portfolio

There are two types of portfolios to choose from: a PDF portfolio and an online portfolio.

The former is a collection of your work samples, which are saved in a PDF format and, therefore, excellent for printing or sharing via email. The latter, however, is more accessible and flexible, allowing you to incorporate videos, animations, and other embedded content.

What Are the Main Elements of a Portfolio?

The main elements of a portfolio may vary depending on your field and goal, but they generally consist of the following:

  • Cover page, which should contain the title, your name, and your location. You don’t have to come up with anything unique; just “Portfolio” will suffice. However, try to make this page visually appealing, as it will be the first thing the reader sees.
  • Project samples, preferably between five and ten of your best works that showcase your skills to the fullest. If you don’t have professional experience, you can include your personal projects, drafts, and ideas to show that you’re creative and can take initiative.
  • Contact information, which should be placed either at the beginning of the portfolio or at the very end. It should contain your email address, location, and links to websites, blogs, or social media.
  • Testimonials, which are not an absolute must, but many professional PDF portfolios include them as social proof. They can make you appear more trustworthy, especially if they come from well-known and respectable professionals in your field.

How to Create a PDF Portfolio in 7 Easy Steps

To help you create a PDF portfolio, we will break down the process into simple steps and illustrate them through real examples.

Here’s what you should do.

Step 1: Choose Your Portfolio Format

Before you start, you should choose your portfolio format, or, in other words, decide between a PDF, a website, or a presentation. All three have their advantages and best use cases, so consider carefully who your audience is and what you want to achieve.

For instance, a PDF portfolio is best for job applications, emails, or interviews that ask you to bring your portfolio with you. However, if your goal is to build your brand or have a greater outreach, an online portfolio is an excellent choice.

Finally, a presentation portfolio is less common but highly effective in certain situations, like when you’re doing client-facing pitches.

Step 2: Gather Your Best Work

Once you’ve chosen your format, it’s time to gather the projects that you plan to include in your portfolio. Don’t overdo it; there’s no need to add more than 10 samples. As a rule, you should select the work you’re most proud of, as well as that which demonstrates your skills the best.

Step 3: Write Clear Project Descriptions

Your work should speak for itself, but that doesn’t mean you shouldn’t introduce it in any way. Context is key for a successful portfolio, and you don’t want the readers to be confused about what they’re actually looking at. That’s why you should describe the sample you’ve included and explain why you’ve decided to showcase it.

There are several ways to do this, but here are a few elements you should consider including in your descriptions:

  • A brief summary
  • A challenge or a hurdle you’ve faced
  • The solution that helped you overcome it

Once the content is prepared, you should design your PDF portfolio by focusing on the layout and the overall appearance. This is particularly important if you’re applying for a field related to the visual arts or design, but you shouldn’t neglect it even if your expertise lies elsewhere.

That’s because a portfolio’s layout and design significantly affect the overall presentation and may contribute to the story you’re trying to tell, or take away from it.

Of course, the most important part is making sure that your work is visible and properly showcased. However, colors, fonts, and element arrangements can’t be overlooked either.

Step 5: Use a Portfolio Builder or Design Tool

Building a portfolio from scratch can take plenty of time, and the final result might still not look as professional as you would want it to. That’s especially true if you don’t have significant experience with design; you’re unlikely to make something impressive on your first try.

Luckily, there are tools with built-in portfolio templates, allowing you to simply select one, add your content, and make necessary adjustments before finalizing. This can save you hours, and the result is usually far more polished and eye-catching.

The most commonly used tool for making PDF portfolios is Adobe Acrobat Pro, where you simply need to select Create > PDF Portfolio to get started. However, this software is pricey, so many people wonder how to create a PDF portfolio without Acrobat.

Another option is an online tool, such as Canva, which also comes with many template options. Go to Templates, type “portfolio” in the search bar, and wait for the results. Hundreds of options will appear, and you just have to scroll through them to find the right one!

Step 6: Convert Your File to PDF

The layout has been chosen, the content added, and now all that’s left is to save the document and convert it to PDF. In some cases, conversion might be unnecessary; for example, Canva allows you to save your project as a PDF immediately.

Step 7: Review and Proofread

Finally, review your PDF portfolio to check if everything is in place and pay particular attention to typos or inconsistencies. This is especially important before submission; no matter how well-designed your portfolio may appear, glaring spelling errors won’t leave a good impression.

4 Design Tips for Creating a Portfolio

Following the right design tips for creating a portfolio can significantly elevate your work and help you leave a lasting impression on your readers. So, here are four things to keep in mind:

  1. Consider the structure. Even when you’ve picked the samples to showcase, your work isn’t done. The order in which you present them is equally important, so think of the impact you want to have. For instance, you may want to slowly build to your best and most impressive piece, or you can follow the rise and fall structure to introduce some variety.
  2. Be harsh with your choices. If you notice that a sample doesn’t fit, even if it was initially picked, leave it out. That may mean you have less work to present, but quality should always take precedence.
  3. Show impact. Whenever you can, include a measurable impact with your samples to show how it benefited your client or the company. For instance, if your redesign of a webpage increased traffic on a particular website, make sure to mention that, along with a precise statistic.
  4. Keep the document scannable. Use high-contrast headers, captivating images, short paragraphs, and bullet points to ensure most information can be understood from scanning. This is important, as most recruiters allocate only a short time to each portfolio.

How to Share Your PDF Portfolio

How you share your PDF portfolio depends on who you want to share it with; some clients and employers prefer receiving these documents as emails, while others may be open to different platforms.

However, before you share your portfolio, make sure to rename your file clearly. Something like 'FirstName_LastName_Portfolio_[year].pdf' makes it easy for recruiters to identify and save your work, especially when reviewing multiple candidates.

If you’re choosing to share it via email, the process is simple: go to your standard email provider, like Gmail or Outlook, compose your email as you usually would, and add it as an attachment. Just make sure that your portfolio doesn’t exceed the size limit, which is 20 MB for most providers. If it does, use our compressor tool to fix this.

Creating a PDF portfolio can boost your chances of getting hired, landing a spot in an academic program, or making a successful client pitch. The most successful portfolios are those that seamlessly blend visual and textual elements to tell a story of your professional development and skills, and this can be difficult to achieve.

How to Create a PDF Portfolio FAQs

#1. How many pages should a portfolio be?

A portfolio should be 10–20 pages long, though this depends on the complexity of your samples. For most professionals, five to ten well-presented projects with concise descriptions will fill this range comfortably without becoming bloated.

#2. Can I create a portfolio in Word and convert it to PDF?

You can create a portfolio in Word and convert it to PDF, and that’s precisely what many professionals do. Word offers all the tools you need to create a polished portfolio, but it may take a bit longer to get it just right. That’s why websites like Canva or Google Docs, which have built-in portfolio templates, are far more popular.

#3. What size should a PDF portfolio be?

Ideally, a portfolio should be under 10 MB, as this guarantees easy sharing via email. Most email providers can handle attachments of up to 20 MB, but large files may still take longer to upload or trigger email filters.
