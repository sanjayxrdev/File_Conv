## Key Takeaways

- A locked PDF **restricts access or editing**, typically through password protection or permission settings.
- **Password-protected PDFs** require a password to open and view, while **permission-restricted PDFs** allow viewing but limit printing, copying, or editing.
- Before editing a locked PDF, you should check what type of password protection is in place and see whether there’s a way to obtain viewing and editing permission.
- **Editing without authorization may be illegal**, especially if the document contains copyrighted or confidential material.
- Common unlocking issues include incorrect passwords, corrupted files, and unsupported encryption types.

## What Does “Locked” Mean in a PDF?

In a PDF, **“locked” means there are restrictions preventing you from fully accessing or editing the file**.

However, not all locked PDFs are the same; generally, there are password-protected PDFs and permission-restricted PDFs.

1. **Password-protected PDFs** require you to enter a password before you can open the file. This is the strongest form of locking because, without the correct password, you can’t even see the content. When you password-protect a PDF, you keep sensitive information secure from unauthorized viewers.
2. **Permission-restricted PDFs**, on the other hand, allow you to open the file but restrict certain actions like printing, copying text, editing, or adding comments. These restrictions are set by the person who created the PDF to control how the content is shared.

[PDF files](https://pdf.net/blog/what-does-pdf-format-mean) are usually locked to protect sensitive data such as contracts, medical records, or financial documents. [Copyright protection](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-protect.html) is another common reason for doing so since publishers may lock PDFs to prevent unauthorized distribution of their work. Finally, locked PDFs help safeguard sensitive information so that only authorized users can access the content.

### Types of Restrictions

The types of restrictions often used for secured PDFs are:

- **Printing restrictions, **which prevent users from printing the PDF or limit the number of pages they can print.
- **Copying restrictions, **which keep users from copying and pasting the PDF’s content.
- **Editing restrictions **that block any changes to the document, such as [direct typing](https://pdf.net/blog/how-do-i-type-on-a-pdf-file), highlighting, or adding fillable fields.
- **Commenting restrictions, **which stop users from adding, editing, or deleting comments and annotations.
- **Form-filling restrictions **that disable the ability to fill out form fields within an [interactive PDF](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-make-pdfs-interactive).

## What to Check Before Editing a Locked PDF

Before editing a locked PDF, you must **check what type of password protection is in place**, as this will determine how to move forward. If the document is locked with a user password, you won’t be able to view the contents at all, while an owner password allows viewing but restricts editing, printing, and commenting.

Here are the basic differences between the two:

| User password (password-protected) | Owner password (password-restricted) |
| --- | --- |
| - Full protection; the document can’t be accessed without a password<br>- Prevents unauthorized viewing and sharing<br>- Third-party apps can remove the protection, but it may not be easy | - Limited protection; the document can’t be changed, copied, or printed without a password<br>- Prevents unauthorized changes and editing<br>- Third-party apps can typically remove these restrictions |

Once you’ve identified the protection type, ensure you have permission to access the document and make changes. If you don’t, it may be illegal to proceed, and you should try to **contact the owner** to avoid potential repercussions.

## Is It Legal to Edit a Locked PDF?

It is legal to edit a locked PDF **when you have the proper rights to do so**. For example, if you are the owner or have been given explicit consent by the owner, unlocking and editing the PDF is perfectly fine.

However, editing a locked PDF without permission may violate copyright laws, terms of use, or confidentiality agreements. If the document contains protected [intellectual property](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/i/intellectualproperty.asp), sensitive information, or is clearly marked as restricted, bypassing its security can be illegal. When in doubt, it is best to **seek permission or legal advice** to avoid potential issues.

## 3 Common Issues When Unlocking PDFs

There are three common issues that you might encounter when unlocking PDFs, and they are as follows:

### #1. Incorrect Password

Many unlocking attempts fail simply because **the password is incorrect**. PDF passwords are case-sensitive and must be exact, including any special characters or spaces. So, if you mistype the password or use an outdated one, the PDF will remain locked.

This problem also often happens if the document owner has given you the wrong password by mistake or changed it to another one. Unfortunately, there is no way to guess or bypass a [password-protected PDF](https://pdf.net/protect-pdf) without the correct one, so you will need to double-check your entry and verify the password with the source if possible.

### #2. Corrupted File

**A corrupted PDF file** can also cause unlocking problems. Corruption can happen while downloading, saving, or transferring the document. It can damage the file so it [won't open properly](https://pdf.net/blog/why-wont-my-pdf-open) or accept the password.

When a PDF is corrupted, your viewer software might crash or fail to unlock it. In these cases, even having the right password won’t help. Repairing a corrupted PDF requires using specialized recovery tools or obtaining a fresh copy from the document owner.

### #3. Unsupported Encryption Type

PDFs can be secured with different levels of encryption, some of which are more complex than others. If your unlocking software **doesn’t support the encryption type used** on the PDF, it won’t be able to remove the restrictions or open the file.

This often happens with proprietary encryption standards, which are usually adopted by businesses, legal firms, and government agencies. To solve this, you may need to use more advanced software, update the one that you are using, or use the official software that created the PDF.

## How to Edit a Locked PDF FAQs

### #1. Why can’t I edit a secured PDF?

You [can’t edit a secured PDF](https://pdf.net/blog/why-cant-i-edit-pdf) because the owner hasn’t given you permission to do so. That may be because they want the document to remain in its original form, as changing the content might compromise it. These kinds of restrictions are common in contracts, intellectual property, legal documents, and other PDFs containing sensitive data.

### #2. How do I edit a PDF that doesn't allow editing?

To edit a PDF that doesn’t allow editing, you can **unlock it with the correct password using pdf.net **and** **then** edit it directly **there**. **Alternatively, you can copy and paste the PDF’s content to Word, make your changes, and then [save it back as a PDF](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-save-as-a-pdf).

### #3. Will unlocking a PDF damage the file?

Unlocking a PDF **will not damage the file if you use reputable software**. There shouldn’t be any [missing text](https://pdf.net/blog/pdf-missing-text) issues, though some complex elements may shift slightly when using certain third-party tools or during conversions.

### #4. What’s the difference between a locked and an encrypted PDF?

The difference between a locked and encrypted PDF is that **a locked PDF has restrictions on actions like editing or printing**, while an encrypted PDF **uses password-based encryption to prevent unauthorized access to its content entirely**. Additionally, some PDFs can be both locked and encrypted.
