Why Is My PDF Printing Blank? 7 Causes and Fast Fixes

Find out why your PDF is printing blank, how to fix this issue, and what to do to prevent it from happening in the future.

Marcus Cooper

Solutions Architect & Workflow Specialist

Table of contents

Your PDF is printing blank because your file may be corrupted, the printer driver and PDF viewer may be outdated, or the font may be unembedded. In these cases, your best bet is to try several fixes and see which one brings the best results.

That said, we’ll explore some of the most common reasons a PDF prints empty pages, as well as potential fixes for each of these issues. Furthermore, we will share what to do to prevent such problems in the future.

Key Takeaways

  • If your PDF is printing blank pages, the issue may be with the file, your printer driver, your PDF reader, fonts and encoding, hidden and transparent layers, a security restriction on the file, or a stuck print queue.
  • You can fix some of these issues by updating your drivers and PDF readers, redownloading the file, or embedding the fonts before exporting a PDF.
  • The best and most versatile solution is to save a PDF as an image or convert it into a JPG before printing.

7 Reasons Why PDFs Print Blank

Reasons your PDFs print blank include a corrupted PDF file, a printer driver issue, problems with PDF viewers, hidden layers or transparent content, font and encoding, security or permission restrictions on the file, or a stuck print queue. Luckily, most of these are fairly easy to fix in just a few minutes.

Now, let’s take a closer look at all these issues and fixes for blank PDFs after printing.

#1. Corrupted or Incomplete PDF File

Printing issues may stem from a corrupted PDF file, which may display normally on your computer, but then fail to render on paper. This often happens because part of a PDF file might not have been downloaded properly from the source, or because it was created with errors in the first place.

How to Fix It

If you suspect this is the cause of your printing issues, it’s best to go back to the source and try redownloading it. Sometimes, the corruption occurs due to an unstable internet connection, so make sure there are no interruptions as you transfer the file.

However, if this doesn’t help, the issue may be with the file itself. In that case, try to contact the owner and request a fixed copy. If this isn’t possible, open the file in a PDF reader like Chrome, Preview, or Adobe Acrobat and use the Print function, then Save as PDF or Microsoft Print to PDF to create an uncorrupted copy.

#2. Printer Driver Issues

Printer driver issues commonly affect PDF printing, as outdated drivers may be unable to establish communication between the file and the device. As a consequence, the output suffers, and the pages come out blank.

How to Fix It

Updating or reinstalling your printer driver is the best solution for this problem. However, first, you need to uninstall your current driver by taking the following steps:

  1. Open Control Panel on your device.
  2. Click on View devices and printers in the Hardware and Sound section.
  3. Scroll to Printers and locate yours, then right-click it and select Remove device.

Once you’ve uninstalled your driver, visit the manufacturer’s page and get the latest driver version. Reinstall it and restart your system to ensure everything configures correctly. After that, try printing the document again.

#3. PDF Viewer Rendering Problems

If your PDF is printing blank or some content is missing, your PDF viewer may be the actual culprit. Some versions, especially older ones, don’t fully support newer PDF formats and advanced features, which can cause printing issues.

How to Fix It

If you suspect your PDF viewer is the issue, there are several ways to address this.

First, if you’re experiencing a blank printing issue with Adobe Acrobat, check for updates to ensure you have the latest version. The process is straightforward: open Adobe, click on Menu, then Help, and finally Check for updates.

The program will run a check and let you install a new version if one is available. After that, try printing again to see if this solves your issue.

Alternatively, you can try several different PDF readers to see if your document prints normally in others. If so, then you’ll know the problem is with a specific reader.

#4. Hidden Layers or Transparent Content in PDF

Hidden layers, transparent content, and visual effects can affect printing and prevent content from appearing on paper. This happens when your printer does not correctly process transparency, layers, or visual effects.

How to Fix It

The simplest fix for hidden content that causes issues is printing the file as an image. This method flattens your PDF by converting it into an image, thus removing all complex formatting and layers.

You can quickly print your PDF file as an image in Adobe Acrobat if you take the following steps:

  1. Click on the Print option from the menu or press CTRL + P on your keyboard.
  2. When the Print menu opens, click on Advanced.
  3. Make sure that the Print as Image checkbox is ticked, then click OK.

After that, follow the usual printing process, and you should have no issues.

#5. Font or Encoding Problems

Missing fonts in PDF printing can cause blank pages; if your fonts aren’t properly embedded, your device might not be able to read them. Embedding essentially ensures the fonts display properly on all devices, while unembedded fonts may be replaced by different symbols or blank spaces.

Similarly, poor encoding can also affect how your devices and readers process the document. Much of the text may not appear on the screen or during printing if it’s not properly encoded when the document was created.

How to Fix It

Since this problem stems from the way your PDF was created in the first place, the best solution is to re-export the document correctly. However, this only works if you have the source file.

If you do, open it in Microsoft Word or whichever program you used to make it, and take the following steps:

  1. Go to File, then Options.
  2. When the new menu opens, go to Save.
  3. Under Preserve fidelity when sharing this document, check the Embed fonts in the file box and click OK.

Then, export the file as a PDF and try printing to see if embedding the fonts brings the desired results.

If you don’t have the source file, your options are to use the print as image PDF fix described above or try Preflight in Adobe Acrobat. This tool is excellent for embedding fonts even after the PDF has been exported, but you can only use it if you own the Pro version.

#6. PDF Security or Permission Restrictions

Some PDFs have security settings that block or limit printing. Depending on your PDF reader, that restriction can show up as a blank or partially blank printout instead of a clear error message telling you printing isn’t allowed.

How to Fix It

Check the file’s security settings. If printing is restricted, you’ll need the password from whoever created the file to unlock the PDF, or an unrestricted copy of the document. There isn’t a legitimate way to remove someone else’s printing restriction without their permission, so the fastest fix is usually to contact the source and ask for an unlocked copy.

#7. Print Queue or Cache Errors

Sometimes the PDF file and your printer are both fine, but a stuck or corrupted print queue produces blank pages anyway. This is more common right after a failed or cancelled print job, or after your printer wakes from sleep mode.

How to Fix It

On Windows, open Services, find Print Spooler, and restart it. If that doesn’t help, stop the service, clear the contents of the print spooler folder, then restart the service and try again.

On a Mac, remove the printer from System Settings, then re-add it to rebuild its queue from scratch. On either system, restarting the printer itself first is worth trying, since that clears its internal buffer too.

How to Check if the PDF Itself Is the Problem

To check if the PDF itself is the problem, consider taking these steps:

  • Open the PDF on multiple devices. If your PDF doesn’t work on any devices or in any programs, the issue is most likely with the file itself. Even if it does open, check whether it prints correctly; if not, the file may still be corrupted.
  • Try copy-pasting text. Select some of the text from the PDF and copy it into a Notepad document. If the text is garbled and confusing, your PDF’s encoding is likely broken.
  • Check file size anomalies. If your PDF is abnormally large or surprisingly small, it may be corrupted. This, paired with other issues, like printing blank or not opening correctly, indicates that something’s wrong with the file.
  • Preview in browser vs. PDF reader. If your PDF opens and prints correctly in a browser but not in a dedicated reader (or vice versa), the issue is likely a rendering or compatibility problem. If the file fails to open or print correctly in both browsers and multiple dedicated readers, the file itself is more likely the problem.

How to Prevent Blank PDF Printing Issues in the Future

To prevent blank PDF printing issues in the future, use the following strategies:

  • Embed the fonts. When saving a new file as a PDF, always turn on the embed fonts feature, as it ensures that your document is readable on all devices. This prevents font-related printing issues, too, so it’s generally a good practice.
  • Keep your printer drivers up to date. Check for new driver versions on the official websites once in a while, and download them to stay up to date. That way, your printer will be able to process modern formatting and complex file structures without a hitch.
  • Update your PDF viewer. Outdated viewers can also cause printing issues, so check for new versions to make sure this doesn’t affect your PDFs. Also, if you work with PDFs frequently, it may be a good idea to install a few readers, so you can easily switch to a different one if your pages start printing blank.
  • Use dedicated software. You can print your PDFs from Chrome, too, but it’s better to download them to your device and use an actual program, like Adobe Acrobat or Preview. This gives you greater control over your printing and more options if something goes wrong.
  • Convert your PDFs to images. Whenever possible, convert your PDFs to JPGs to flatten them and prepare them for printing. This way, you’ll circumvent the entire issue of complex formatting or layers not printing correctly.

Why Is My PDF Printing Blank FAQs

#1. Can a corrupted PDF cause blank printing?

Yes, a corrupted PDF can cause blank printing, and it’s one of the most common culprits, too. Corruption often occurs as you download or transfer the file, so all you need to do is get it from the source again and make sure your connection doesn’t get interrupted.

#2. Why does Chrome print blank PDFs?

Chrome may print blank PDFs because of outdated printer drivers, corrupted cache, or conflicts with the hardware acceleration feature. You can fix these issues by updating your drivers, clearing the cache, and toggling off hardware acceleration in Chrome’s settings.

#3. How do I fix Adobe printing blank pages?

You can fix Adobe printing blank pages by checking if you have the latest version of the software installed and updating it if necessary. You can also go to Help > Fix installation to repair potentially corrupted versions of Adobe that may be causing your issues.

#4. Can a print queue error cause my PDF to print blank?

Yes, a stuck or corrupted print queue can cause blank pages even when the PDF file itself is fine. Restarting the Print Spooler service on Windows, or removing and re-adding the printer on Mac, usually clears it.
