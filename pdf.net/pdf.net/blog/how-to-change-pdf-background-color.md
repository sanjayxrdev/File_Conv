## Key Takeaways

- Changing a PDF’s background color can improve **readability, accessibility, branding**, and help **reduce printing costs**.
- You can change the background color with **Adobe Acrobat Pro**, **online PDF editors**, or by converting the file in **Microsoft Word** or **Google Docs**.

## Why Is It Necessary to Change a PDF’s Background Color?

It is sometimes necessary to change the background color of a document because it influences PDF accessibility, your branding, or printing. Even though it might seem like a small detail, the color can **make a significant difference **in how your PDF is used and perceived.

First, from an accessibility standpoint, adjusting the background color **improves readability** for people with visual impairments or [sensitivity to light](https://www.uclahealth.org/medical-services/ophthalmology/eye-signs-and-symptoms/light-sensitivity). A softer background can reduce [eye strain](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/21059-eye-strain), provide stronger contrast, and make it easier to follow the content.

Also, color is one of the most common components of [branding](https://brandingstrategyinsider.com/2013/07/the-importance-of-color-in-brand-strategy/). Beyond aesthetics, it influences perception, evokes emotions, and communicates values, making color-branded PDFs feel more professional and reliable. This visual cohesiveness not only **strengthens brand recall** but also **builds trust **and creates a memorable experience for audiences.

Lastly, **printing** considerations can influence the choice of a PDF’s background color. Lighter tones may** reduce ink consumption** and **lower** **costs** for large print runs, which is important when working with PDF e-books, forms, and marketing materials.

## 3 Easy Methods to Change a PDF’s Background Color

Below are three easy methods to change a PDF's background color, each with its own advantages and limitations:

### #1. Using Adobe Acrobat Pro

With** Adobe Acrobat Pro**, you can change the PDF background color as well as customize the color's opacity and play around with options, such as adding an image as the background.

Follow these steps to edit your PDF’s background:

- Open your PDF with Adobe Acrobat Pro.
- Find the global bar in the upper-left corner and click **Edit**.
- Click **More** under **Add Content**, then **Background > Add**.
- Choose the **Source**, **Appearance**, and **Position** of the background.
- Click** OK** to apply the background.

However, note that this PDF background color change method requires a **subscription**; it is worth it if you regularly work with PDFs. However, if you only need to make occasional edits, a free [online PDF editor](https://pdf.net/blog/best-pdf-editors) or a conversion through Microsoft Word or Google Docs may be a better choice.

### #2. Using Online PDF Editors

Online PDF editors offer an accessible way to change background colors without the need of any software installation. They are especially useful if you only need to make small edits, such as [changing text](https://pdf.net/change-text-in-pdf), or don’t have access to premium programs like Adobe Acrobat Pro.

However, because they are browser-based, they pose more risks unless you work exclusively with non-sensitive PDFs. To determine if an [online PDF editor is safe](https://pdf.net/blog/are-online-pdf-editors-safe), it is best to check for HTTPS connections and clear privacy policies.

## How to Change PDF Background Color for Accessibility

You can use **built-in features** in PDF editors or **accessibility settings** on your device to change the background color of a PDF for accessibility.

For example, creating a high-contrast PDF with light text on a dark background makes on-screen reading more comfortable. Some editors allow you to **set custom color schemes** or **apply changes across multiple pages** for consistency.

The key to accessibility is not only the background color itself but also **how it interacts with the text**. Poor combinations can make content difficult to read, while the right pairings improve the user's experience.

These are the main tips to** keep in mind**:

- Avoid red/green or blue/purple pairings, which are difficult for color-blind users
- Use a high contrast, such as black text on a white background or white text on black
- Check color contrast ratios with accessibility tools to meet [compliance standards](https://www.section508.gov/create/pdfs/)
- Choose softer background tones, like light gray, to reduce glare
- Make sure links, headings, and lists still stand out

## What to Avoid When Changing PDF Background Color

Below are some of the most important things to **avoid** when changing a PDF's background color:

- **Using overly bright or clashing colors. **For example, neon or highly saturated colors; these look eye-catching but reduce readability because they cause eye strain. Instead, you choose softer tones or high-contrast combinations that make the text stand out clearly without overwhelming the reader.
- **Forgetting to check all pages or layers. **Many PDFs contain multiple layers or design elements. So, if you only update the main pages, hidden layers or background sections may still show the old color. This can result in an inconsistent look that distracts readers and makes the document appear unprofessional. That said, be sure to always review your whole file.
- **Not saving a copy. **Editing directly on your only version risks data loss. Therefore, make it a habit to save a backup copy before you make any changes. This way, you still have an original version that you can re-duplicate if formatting shifts, colors do not display as expected, or [PDF missing text](https://pdf.net/blog/pdf-missing-text) issues occur.

## How to Change a PDF’s Background Color FAQs

### #1. Can I change the background color of a scanned PDF?

You** **can change the background color of a scanned PDF after running it through an OCR tool, which makes it editable. However, unlike a regular PDF, [editing scanned PDFs](https://pdf.net/blog/how-to-edit-scanned-pdfs) requires additional steps.

### #2. Will changing the background color affect printing quality?

Changing the background color of a PDF can affect the printing quality **if you don’t choose an appropriate color**. Dark or saturated backgrounds may use more ink, while lighter colors generally print more efficiently. To ensure the printing quality meets your expectations, **preview** a sample page **before printing** large volumes.

### #3. Can I change the PDF background color on some pages only?

**Yes, you can** change the PDF background color on some pages only. Simply specify a page range or work on each page at a time to leave certain pages in their original colors.

### #4. Can I set a custom image or gradient instead of a solid background color in a PDF?

**Yes, you can **set a custom image or gradient instead of a solid background color in a PDF using **Adobe Acrobat Pro** and **Microsoft** **Word**. Some** advanced online PDF editors** may also offer this option.

### #5. Will changing the background color increase the file size of my PDF?

Changing the background color will not increase the file size of your PDF** by too much**. If you use more complex backgrounds, such as those with images, patterns, or gradients, your file size **may increase a bit more** than with solid colors.
