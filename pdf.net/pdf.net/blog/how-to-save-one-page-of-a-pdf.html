How to Save One Page of a PDF: 3 Simple Methods to Consider

You can easily save one page of a PDF by extracting it with pdf.net, converting from another file format, or creating it from scratch.

There are three ways you can save one page of a PDF: extracting it from an existing PDF, converting it from a different file format, or creating it from scratch.

You might want to do this to share a specific section of a document, print one sheet without wasting paper, or isolate content for editing. A single-page PDF is smaller and more focused, so it is also easier to upload, store, and manage.

In this guide, we will explain these methods in detail, including when to choose each one, and share tips to help you optimize your final file.

Key Takeaways

  • If you have an existing PDF and want to save just one of its pages, you can use the Split PDF tool to extract your single page and download it as a PDF.
  • You can also convert single-page files from other formats (e.g., DOCX, JPG, PNG, etc.) into PDF.
  • The third option is to create a new page from scratch and save or export it as a PDF.
  • Choosing your fonts carefully, compressing images before inserting them, previewing your PDF on different devices, and checking the final file size before distribution help you optimize your single-page PDF.

3 Methods to Make One Page of a PDF

Below are three practical ways to make a one-page PDF, depending on what you are starting with.

#1. Extract a Single Page from an Existing PDF

If you already have a multi-page PDF and only need one of its pages, extracting is the best way to go about it. Instead of recreating the content or manually copying and pasting, you can simply isolate the page you need and save it as a PDF.

This method is especially useful when you only need one specific section from a longer document; for example, sharing just the takeaways of a long PDF report. Saving one page of a PDF ensures your recipient doesn’t have to scroll for a long time to get what they need.

It’s also helpful when you want to reduce file size, as sending a single page instead of a full multi-page PDF makes attachments lighter and quicker to upload. This is important when you want to submit files through portals with strict size limits or share PDFs over slower Internet connections.

#2. Convert Other Formats to a Single PDF Page

Converting other formats to a single PDF page is the most practical option when your content is still in Word, PowerPoint, Excel, or an image format.

However, a converter will preserve your layout as it is, so before you move forward with the process, you should take the time to adjust margins, font size, spacing, or page breaks and ensure nothing spills onto the second page.

Rather than having to shuffle through other unreliable PDF tools online, you can head straight to our PDF editor to convert files from Word, JPEG, and PNG into PDF format in just a few clicks.

A converter is handy when you don’t have access to the original editing software or when you are working across different devices. It allows you to turn the file into a PDF without relying on a specific program, so the process is simple and accessible.

#3. Create a Single-Page PDF from Scratch

Creating a single-page PDF from scratch gives you complete control over the content and layout. You should begin with a blank document in your preferred platform and set the page size first, so you know exactly what you are working with.

Then, it is important to structure your content carefully and ensure everything fits on one page without looking cramped. Setting consistent spacing between paragraphs and headings and using white space to separate ideas can make your content easier to scan.

Once you are happy with your document, export it as a PDF. Here is how you can do that for Word, Excel, and PowerPoint:

Word

  1. Select File from the top toolbar.
  2. Click Save as… in the dropdown.
  3. Choose PDF under Export formats in the dropdown menu.
  4. Pick Best for electronic distribution… or Best for printing.
  5. Press Export.

Excel

  1. Click File from the toolbar at the top of the page.
  2. Press Save as… from the dropdown options.
  3. Choose PDF as the file format.
  4. Tick Workbook or Sheet.
  5. Select Export.

PowerPoint

  1. Choose File in the toolbar.
  2. Pick Save as… from the options that appear.
  3. Change the file format from PPT to PDF.
  4. Click Export.

4 Tips for Optimizing Your Single-Page PDF

After getting your single-page PDF, you can take a few extra steps to optimize it by:

  • Choosing fonts carefully. The fonts you choose can directly affect how easy it is for users to read your content. If they are currently harsh on the eyes, you should test out options on both large and small screens. Change to a font that allows users to go through your PDF without zooming in and out.
  • Compressing images. You should compress images before inserting them into your PDF to avoid enlarging the overall file size. Larger files take longer to upload, download, and load, frustrating users and potentially impacting search engine rankings if you embed PDFs on websites.
  • Previewing your PDF on different devices. Text that looks well-spaced on a large screen may appear cramped on a phone because of the non-responsive nature of PDFs. Reviewing it across devices helps ensure formatting stays consistent and the document remains easy to read for all users.
  • Checking the final file size before sending or sharing. If it is too large, use our Compress PDF tool to make it more manageable. You won’t have to worry about attachment limits, slow uploads, or users struggling to open the PDF on their devices after compression.
