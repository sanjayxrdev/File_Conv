How to Make a PDF on iPhone: 4 Quick Methods

Learn how to make a PDF on iPhone from photos, files, web pages, and notes using built-in apps or our quick online PDF creator.

Technical Content Specialist

Table of contents

Making a PDF on iPhone only takes a few taps, since Apple handles PDF creation directly within the Photos, Files, Notes, and Safari apps. You don't need to download a separate iPhone PDF creator to turn a picture, document, or web page into a shareable file.

Knowing how to do this comes in handy more often than you'd think, be it saving a receipt, sending a signed form, or archiving an article to read later. A PDF also keeps your file’s layout intact no matter which device someone opens it on.

This guide covers four ways to create a PDF on iPhone, from photos, files, web pages, and notes, plus a few tips for keeping your finished files clean and easy to share.

Key Takeaways

  • You can make a PDF on iPhone directly from Photos, Files, Notes, or Safari, without installing any third-party app.
  • The Files app's Quick Actions menu turns one photo or several into a PDF in seconds.
  • Safari lets you save any web page as a PDF straight from the Share menu.
  • The Notes app converts scanned documents or typed notes into a PDF automatically.

How to Make a PDF on iPhone from Photos

The fastest way to make a PDF from photos on iPhone is to use the Files app's built-in Quick Actions menu. Here are the steps to follow:

1. Open the Photos app and select the images you want to convert.

2. Tap the Share icon and choose Save to Files.

3. Open the Files app, tap and hold the image, then select Quick Actions.

4. Tap Create PDF, and iOS will generate the file instantly.

This method works well for a single image, but if you need to create a PDF from photos on iPhone in bulk, resize them, or add pages afterward, you may want to consider other options.

How to Create a PDF from Files on iPhone

You can also make a document PDF on iPhone straight from an existing file, like a Word document, Pages file, or spreadsheet, using the Print function. Here's how:

1. Open the file you want to convert in its original app.

2. Tap the Share icon, then tap Print.

3. In the print preview, pinch outward on the page to open it as a PDF.

4. Tap the Share icon again and choose Save to Files.

This trick works for almost any app with a Print option, which makes it one of the most reliable ways to save as a PDF on iPhone without installing anything extra.

How to Save a Web Page as a PDF on iPhone

Next, you can save a web page as a PDF on iPhone in Safari, which can do it without requiring a separate iPhone PDF creator.

Follow these steps to convert to PDF on iPhone straight from your browser:

1. Open the page you want to save in Safari.

2. Tap the Share icon at the bottom of the screen.

3. Tap Options near the top of the share sheet, select PDF, then tap the checkmark.

4. Tap Save to Files to store the finished PDF.

This is a quick way to keep a copy of an article, receipt, or booking confirmation. Apple's own guide to saving webpages as a PDF in Safari covers a few extra markup options if you also want to highlight or annotate the page first.

The Notes app is the easiest way to create a PDF on iPhone from a scanned document, since it captures, crops, and saves pages as a PDF in one flow. Here's how:

1. Open the Notes app and tap the Clip icon in a new or existing note.

2. Tap Scan Documents and hold your iPhone over the page you want to capture.

3. Adjust the corners if the scan doesn't fit the page correctly, then tap Keep Scan.

4. Tap the dropdown menu in the top-left corner and select Save to Files. iOS will automatically convert the scan into a PDF.

An option like this is ideal for signed forms, receipts, or handwritten pages. If you want to take a closer look at the process, Apple also has an official guide to scanning documents on iPhone that covers every step in detail.

5 Tips for Creating Better PDFs on iPhone

Once you know how to make a PDF on iPhone, you can adopt some habits that can keep your files easier to see in PDF readers, share, and store. These five tips help you avoid bloated files, missing pages, and lost originals:

#1. Crop Pages Before Saving

Cropping trims out empty margins, background clutter, or stray fingers caught in a scan. Both the Notes app and Files app let you drag the corners of a scan before you save it, so you can take a few extra seconds to line up the edges. A tightly cropped page both looks more professional and keeps the resulting PDF smaller, since there's less image data to store.

#2. Combine Related Files

If you're creating several PDFs on the same topic, like a multi-page contract or a set of receipts, it's better to combine them into a single document instead of sending separate attachments.

The Notes app lets you add multiple scans to one note, and once you're back at a computer, you can use pdf.net's merge PDF tool to combine files that were created separately, even if some started as Word documents or images.

#3. Compress Large PDFs

Photo-heavy PDFs can get large fast, especially if you're combining several scans. Large files take longer to send by email or message and can hit attachment limits. If your file feels unusually large, you can run it through a compression tool before sending it, since it reduces the file size while preserving acceptable quality.

#4. Keep Originals Backed Up

Before you convert or compress a document, save a copy of the original file, be it the source photo, Word document, or scan. Storing it in iCloud Drive or another cloud folder means you can always recreate the PDF from scratch if you need a different format, a higher-resolution version, or want to make edits down the line.

#5. Make Sure It Displays Well on Any Device

A PDF that looks fine on your iPhone can sometimes render oddly on a laptop or older tablet. Before sharing an important document, it’s best to open it on another device if you can, or follow a few mobile-friendly PDF formatting tips to keep text legible and pages properly sized across screens.

Final Thoughts

Making a PDF on iPhone doesn't require any special software. Between the Photos, Files, Notes, and Safari apps, you already have everything you need to turn pictures, documents, and web pages into shareable PDFs in just a few taps.

How to Make a PDF on iPhone FAQ

#1. Can I turn multiple photos into one PDF on iPhone?

Yes, you can turn multiple photos into one PDF on iPhone. Select all the images in Photos before tapping Share, or add several photos to a single note in the Notes app. Both methods combine the images into one multi-page file instead of separate documents.

#2. Where are PDFs saved on iPhone?

PDFs created on iPhone are saved wherever you choose in the Files app, usually in iCloud Drive or On My iPhone, unless you select a different folder during the save step. You can find them again by opening Files and checking Recents or the folder you originally picked.

#3. Can I create a PDF from an email on iPhone?

Yes, you can create a PDF from an email on iPhone by opening the message in the Mail app, tapping the reply arrow, and selecting Print. From the print preview, pinch outward to open the PDF view, then tap the Share icon to save it to Files or send it elsewhere.

#4. Do I need an app to make a PDF on iPhone?

No, you don't need a third-party app to make a PDF on iPhone. The Photos, Files, Notes, and Safari apps all include built-in PDF creation, so native iOS tools cover most everyday needs without any extra downloads.
