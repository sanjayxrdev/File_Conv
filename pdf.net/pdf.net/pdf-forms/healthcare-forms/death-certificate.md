## Facts

### What Is a Death Certificate Form?

A death certificate form is an official document recording the date, time, and cause of death and serving as proof that an individual has passed away. It is used for settling the deceased’s affairs (transferring assets, closing accounts) and obtaining life insurance or retirement benefits. Death certificates are also vital for legal and medical investigations.

### Who Is Required to Fill Out a Death Certificate Form?

Death certificates are filled out by physicians and funeral directors; the former complete the medical portion of the certificate, while the latter fill out demographic sections. If a death is suspicious or the cause is unknown, a coroner may complete the medical sections instead.

### When Should a Death Certificate Form Be Submitted?

A death certificate is submitted when a person passes away, and it’s typically first filed with the local or state vital records office within 72 hours of death. However, this process can last longer depending on the circumstances (for instance, if there’s an ongoing investigation into the cause).

### What Are the Penalties for Providing Incorrect Information?

Providing incorrect information on a death certificate is considered a felony and may result in significant fines and imprisonment. The exact sentence varies from state to state, but penalties may include fines of up to $10,000 and imprisonment for up to 10 years.

### How Do I Submit a Death Certificate Form?

Medical examiners and funeral directors submit death certificate forms to the vital records office of the state where the death occurred. To do so, they must obtain the necessary information about the deceased from the family and complete the form as instructed.
