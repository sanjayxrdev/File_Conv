What Is the VA Form 21-10210?

VA Form 21-10210, sometimes called a “buddy statement,” is a document submitted in support of a veteran’s claim to particular benefits. This form is usually completed by family members or other veterans familiar with the situation, but the claimant can also write their personal statement.

Who Should Use the VA Form 21-10210?

Anyone who knows the claimant’s situation, including the claimant themselves, can use VA Form 21-10210. This encompasses family members, other veterans, coworkers, and friends.

When Do You File the VA Form 21-10210?

You should file VA Form 21-10210 whenever you need to support a particular claim, either at the same time as you submit the main document or a little after that. Furthermore, you can file it to appeal an unfavorable decision or a denied claim.

What Are the Penalties For Not Filing the VA Form 21-10210?

There are no penalties for not filing VA Form 21-10210, though you risk having your claim denied due to insufficient evidence. However, fraudulent claims are punishable by fines or imprisonment, depending on their severity.

Where Do I File the VA Form 21-10210?

You should file VA Form 21-10210 with the Department of Veterans Affairs, using the address provided on the official website. You can also use the VA online tool to submit it electronically.

You can correct mistakes on VA Form 21-10210 by submitting a new, updated document or sending a letter explaining the corrections that need to be made. If you’ve made multiple significant errors, we recommend the former.

#7. How long does it take to process VA Form 21-10210?

VA Form 21-10210 isn’t processed separately, but rather as part of the package with your main form. As a result, you won’t get any notice when its processing is completed, and you’ll only be informed of your claim’s outcome.

#8. Who can sign the VA Form 21-10210?

VA Form 21-10210 can be signed by the veteran, claimant, or witness, depending on who fills out the form and for whom.

VA Form 21-10210 is a supporting document for the main claim, so it’s not a requirement to attach other documents to it. However, you can submit it along with medical records and other evidence that strengthens your claims.

#10. Can a representative help me fill out this form?

Yes, a representative can help you fill out this form, but they should be VA-accredited. For instance, Veterans Service Organizations (VSOs) may be a good choice.
