## Facts

### What Is the Form 4972?

Form 4972 is used to figure your taxes on a qualified lump-sum distribution received from a pension or profit-sharing fund. Since you can choose to be taxed using the 20% capital gain election, the 10-year tax option, or both, your tax liability may be lower than if you reported the distribution as regular income.

### Who Should Use the Form 4972?

Individuals who receive qualified lump-sum distributions and are born before 2nd January 1936 can use Form 4972 to report and calculate taxes. Furthermore, after their deaths, their beneficiaries should fill out this document if they received the distribution in their name.

### When Do You File the Form 4972?

You should file Form 4972 with your federal income tax return (Form 1040), which is due annually on 15th April. However, Form 4972 isn’t recurring; you submit it only for the year when you receive the distribution.

### What Are the Penalties For Not Filing the Form 4972?

There are no specific penalties for not filing Form 4972, as you can report your lump-sum distribution as regular income and pay taxes accordingly. However, you might lose an opportunity to lower your liability in that case.

### Where Do I File the Form 4972?

You should file Form 4972 with the IRS, either by mail or electronically. Whatever you choose, remember to attach it to Form 1040 and send it as a package.
