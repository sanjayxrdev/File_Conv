## Facts

### What Is Schedule C (Form 1040)?

Schedule C (Form 1040) is a document used to report income and losses from a business you operate or a profession you practice as a sole proprietor. A qualifying activity is any activity that you conduct continuously and regularly to make a profit.

### Who Is Required to File Schedule C (Form 1040)?

Schedule C (Form 1040) is required for sole proprietors who operate a business or practice a profession and need to report business income or losses. It also applies to statutory employees (if their W-2 indicates Schedule C eligibility), spouses in a qualified joint venture who elect to file separately, and independent contractors or freelancers who earn self-employment income. Anyone who receives business income that isn’t reported on a W-2 typically must file Schedule C.

### When is Schedule C (Form 1040) Due?

Schedule C (Form 1040) is due by April 15th, just like the principal Form 1040. If this date falls on a weekend or a national holiday, the deadline is pushed to the next business day.

### What Are the Penalties For Not Filing Schedule C (Form 1040)?

Not filing Schedule C (Form 1040) means that you haven’t properly completed your federal income tax return, so penalties can be significant. First, there is a failure-to-file penalty of 5% of unpaid tax per month. Then, you may also face a 0.5% failure-to-pay penalty. On top of that, unpaid taxes accrue interest, so your liability increases with time.

### Where Do I File Schedule C (Form 1040)?

You should file Schedule C (Form 1040) with the IRS, but not as a standalone document. This document is submitted as part of the Form 1040 package, which can be filed electronically or in paper form. E-filing is faster and, therefore, recommended.
