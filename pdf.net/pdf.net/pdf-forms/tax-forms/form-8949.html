A Form 8949 is an IRS form used to report sales and exchanges of capital assets and reconcile them with Forms 1099. You can get an editable Form 8949 to complete online with our easy-to-use editor tool.

What Is a Form 8949 Used For?

A Form 8949 is used to report each sale or exchange of a capital asset, including stocks, real estate, and digital assets. It also reconciles amounts reported on Forms 1099-B, 1099-DA, or 1099-S with what you report on your return, including any adjustments.

Who Should Use a Form 8949?

You should use a Form 8949 if you sold or disposed of capital assets during the year. It applies to individuals, businesses, and trusts, including reporting items like nonbusiness bad debts, worthless securities, and certain investment elections.

When Is the Form 8949 Due?

The Form 8949 is due with your tax return. If you file an extension, the deadline is extended, but you must still report your transactions accurately.

What Happens If a Form 8949 Is Not Submitted?

If your Form 8949 is not submitted, the IRS may identify mismatches between your return and reported 1099 data. This can result in notices, processing delays, or penalties if gains or losses are not properly reported.

How and Where Do I File a Form 8949?

You file the Form 8949 with Schedule D as part of your federal tax return. It can be submitted electronically or by mail to the IRS. Totals from your Form 8949 must be carried over to Schedule D to calculate your overall capital gain or loss.
