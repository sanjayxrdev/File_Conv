## Facts

### What Is the Form W-3?

Form W-3 is known as the Transmittal of Wage and Tax Statements, which is precisely what it is used for: filing Copy A of Forms W-2. This document is never submitted on its own, as it has no purpose outside of transmitting Forms W-2.

### Who Should Use Form W-3?

Individuals filing Form W-2 should use Form W-3 to summarize and transmit their wage and tax statements, detailing the income received and taxes withheld.

### When Is the Form W-3 Due?

Form W-2 is due on the same day as Form W-2, that is, the 31st of January of the following year. However, if this date falls on a weekend or a national holiday, the deadline is pushed to the next business day.

### What Are the Penalties For Not Filing the Form W-3?

Penalties for not filing Form W-3 actually arise from the associated W-2 Forms, which won’t be accepted and processed without it, unless you’re e-filing. The fines are as follows:

- $60 per Form W-2 if you file it within 30 days after the due date
- $130 per Form W-2 if you submit it more than 30 days after the due date, but before 1st August
- $340 per Form W-2 if you file after 1st August or don’t file at all

### Where Do I File the Form W-3?

You should file Form W-3 with the Social Security Administration (SSA), either electronically or by sending it by mail. Use the official SSA platform for e-filing, or send your package to one of the designated addresses.
