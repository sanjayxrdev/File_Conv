## Facts

### What Is the Form 1096?

Form 1096 is used to transmit specific paper forms, or more precisely, Forms 1097, 1098, 1099, 3921, 3922, 5498, W-2G, and their subtypes, to the IRS. However, it shouldn’t be filed when submitting these documents electronically.

### Who Is Required to Fill Out Form 1096?

The individual or entity that files any of the abovementioned forms and their subtypes must fill out Form 1096 when sending them by mail.

### When Is the Form 1096 Due?

Form 1096 is due at the same time as the main form. So, for example, it should be filed by February 28th with Forms 1097, 1098, 1099, 3921, 3922, and W-2G, by January 31st with Form 1099-NEC, and by May 31st with Forms 5498. If any of these dates fall on a weekend or a national holiday, the deadline is pushed to the next business day.

### What Are the Penalties for Not Submitting the Form 1096?

The consequence of not submitting Form 1096 is that the IRS will reject your information return as incorrect and potentially penalize you for late submission, even if you file it correctly afterward. These are the penalties you may face:

- $60 per information return if you file it within 30 days after the deadline
- $130 per information return if you file it more than 30 days after the deadline but before August 1st
- $340 per information return if you file it after August 1st, or don’t at all

### Where Do I File the Form 1096?

You should file Form 1096 with the IRS, along with the principal form that needs to be transmitted. The package should be sent to a designated address that depends on your location.
