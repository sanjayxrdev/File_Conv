# Form 1041

Category: Tax Forms

Form 1041 is used by fiduciaries to report the income and deductions of a deceased person’s trust or estate for tax purposes.

## Facts

### What Is Form 1041 Used For?

Form 1041 is used to report the income, deductions, gains, and losses of a decedent’s trust or estate, as well as each beneficiary’s share of income. This is done for tax purposes, since trusts and shares have the same liability as individuals.

### Who Should Use Form 1041?

Form 1041 is used by fiduciaries; that is, trustees, executors, or administrators of a domestic estate or trust that generates at least $600 in gross income during a tax year. The form must also be completed if any of the beneficiaries aren’t residents of the U.S.

### When Is the Form 1041 Due?

The due date for Form 1041 depends on whether it’s being filed for a calendar-year or a fiscal-year trust or estate. For the former, the deadline is April 15th, the same day by which individuals must file their income tax returns.



However, fiscal-year trusts or estates must submit Form 1041 by the 15th day of the 4th month following the end of their tax year. So, if their tax year ends on June 30th, they must file the return by October 15th.

### What Happens If Form 1041 Is Not Submitted?

If Form 1041 isn’t submitted, the IRS imposes failure-to-file penalties: 5% of the tax due for each month, with a maximum of 25%. Moreover, unpaid taxes accrue interest, which further increases your liability. Finally, if the return is more than 60 days late, the penalty is the smaller of $525 or the tax due.

### How and Where Do I File Form 1041?

You should file Form 1041 with the IRS, using one of the addresses provided on the official website. It’s also possible to file this document electronically, but you must first apply to become an e-file provider and pass a suitability check.
