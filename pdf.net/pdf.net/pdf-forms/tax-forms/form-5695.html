IRS Form 5695 is a document used to claim residential energy credits for solar, wind, geothermal, and qualifying home upgrades.

Because federal residential energy tax credits were largely terminated for property placed in service after December 31, 2025, timing matters.

What Is Form 5695 Used For?

IRS Form 5695 is used to calculate and claim residential energy credits, including the Residential Clean Energy Credit for solar, wind, geothermal, and battery storage, and the Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit for insulation, windows, doors, and heat pumps installed at your main home.

Who Should Use Form 5695?

Taxpayers who made qualifying energy-saving improvements to their main home during 2025 should use Form 5695. This includes anyone who installed solar panels, geothermal systems, energy-efficient windows, or heat pumps. It’s recommended to check the Form 5695 instructions for exact eligibility requirements before you file.

When Is Form 5695 Due?

The Form 5695 due date matches your federal income tax return deadline, typically April 15th, or October 15th with an extension. Since the OBBBA ended these credits for property placed in service after December 31st, 2025, only qualifying 2025 installations can still be claimed.

What Happens If Form 5695 Is Not Submitted?

Not submitting Form 5695 means losing the residential energy credit for that year, but there's no separate penalty for the form itself. You typically have three years from your filing deadline to amend a return and claim a missed credit.

How and Where Do I File Form 5695?

You file Form 5695 alongside Form 1040, attaching it to report your credit on Schedule 3. It can be e-filed through the IRS or your tax software, or the physical form can be mailed to the IRS address listed for your state in the Form 1040 instructions.

#2. Can I submit Form 5695 electronically?

Yes, you can submit Form 5695 electronically when you e-file your Form 1040 through the IRS Free File program or commercial tax software. Electronic filing is typically processed faster than a paper return submitted by mail and reduces the chance of errors. #3. What if my Form 5695 is missing information?

If your Form 5695 is missing information, like a QMID for Part II property, the IRS may delay or reduce your credit. Double-check every entry against your receipts and manufacturer certifications, or use a Form 5695 example as a reference before you submit your return, so nothing holds up your refund.

#6. Can I correct mistakes on Form 5695?

Yes, you can correct mistakes on Form 5695 by filing an amended return, Form 1040-X, along with a revised Form 5695 attached. This is common if you discover missing receipts, an incorrect QMID, or a miscalculated credit amount after you've already submitted your original tax return. Also, keep your documentation handy in case the IRS requests it.

#7. How long does it take to process Form 5695?

Processing Form 5695 typically follows your overall tax return timeline, which is about 21 days for e-filed returns with direct deposit, or more than six weeks for paper returns. Errors, missing QMIDs, or incomplete Part II worksheets can extend processing and delay your residential energy credit refund, while filing early in the season generally speeds things up.

#8. Can I carry forward unused credits from Form 5695?

You can still carry forward unused Residential Clean Energy Credit (Part I) to future tax years if it exceeds your tax liability. However, the Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (Part II) has no carryforward; because both credits generally end after December 31, 2025, it’s worth planning qualifying 2025 improvements carefully.
