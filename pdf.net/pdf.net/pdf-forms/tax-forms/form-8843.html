What Is Form 8843 Used For?

IRS Form 8843 is used to explain why certain days of physical presence in the United States should be excluded when applying the substantial presence test. Alien individuals may need to file it if they qualify as exempt individuals (such as certain students, teachers, trainees, foreign-government-related individuals, or professional athletes) or if a medical condition prevented them from leaving the United States.

Who Should Use Form 8843?

Certain F, J, M, and Q visa holders who are nonresident aliens for tax purposes should use Form 8843, as well as their spouses and dependents. F-1 students holding a Form I-20 typically qualify as exempt individuals, and every qualifying family member files a separate form.

When Is Form 8843 Due?

The Form 8843 due date is April 15 if you attach it to Form 1040-NR, or June 15 if you file it alone with no U.S. income. There's no separate extension beyond your attached return's deadline.

What Happens If Form 8843 Is Not Submitted?

If Form 8843 isn’t submitted, the IRS may count your excluded days toward the substantial presence test. That reclassifies you as a resident alien, exposing worldwide income to U.S. tax and may affect how the IRS determines your U.S. tax residency status, but there are no monetary Form 8843 penalties.

How and Where Do I File Form 8843?

If you are required to file Form 1040-NR, you file Form 8843 by attaching it to the return and submitting them together using the filing method and address provided in the Form 1040-NR instructions. The mailing address may vary depending on whether you are including a tax payment.

Yet, if you are not required to file a U.S. income-tax return, you mail the document separately to the IRS address in Austin, Texas, shown in the Form 8843instructions for the applicable tax year.

#1. How do I get a blank Form 8843?

You can get a blank Form 8843 by opening our fillable version online and starting from a clean template. This way, you don’t need to waste time searching through the IRS’s database.

#2. Can I submit Form 8843 electronically?

No, you cannot submit Form 8843 electronically on its own. If you're filing a Form 1040-NR through approved e-file software, the software can transmit Form 8843 as an attachment. Standalone filers without a tax return must print and mail their Form 8843 to the IRS service center address listed in the instructions.

#3. What if my Form 8843 is missing information?

If your Form 8843 is missing information, go back, fix the fields that need modifications, and review the instructions or a Form 8843 example before submitting to confirm everything is accurate. An incomplete form can delay processing or fail to properly exclude your exempt days, so double-check your visa dates, institution details, and signature before mailing it to the IRS.

#4. Can I fill out my Form 8843 online?

Yes, you can fill out Form 8843 online using our editable PDF tool instead of printing a blank copy by hand. Type your visa details, exemption category, and other required information directly into the form fields, then download or print the finished document for signing and mailing to the IRS.

#5. Is this form free?

#6. Can I correct mistakes on Form 8843?

Yes, you can correct mistakes on Form 8843 before you mail it by editing the fields in our editable Form 8843 tool and reprinting a clean copy. If you already mailed an incorrect form, the IRS generally recommends submitting a corrected one with a brief note explaining the requested change.

#7. How long does it take to process Form 8843?

The IRS doesn't publish a formal processing timeline for Form 8843 because it’s an informational statement, not a tax return. When attached to Form 1040-NR, it processes alongside your return, which typically takes several weeks; standalone filings are simply kept on file for IRS records.

#8. Is Form 8843 considered a tax return?

No, Form 8843 is not considered a tax return. It's an informational statement that nonresident aliens file to explain which days of U.S. presence they can exclude from the substantial presence test, separate from any income reporting done on a Form 1040-NR or Form 1040.

#9. Do F1 students need to file Form 8843?

Yes, F1 students need to file Form 8843 every year they're present in the U.S. as exempt individuals, even without income, for up to five calendar years. If you also earn U.S. income and want to claim tax treaty benefits, you may need to file a W-8BEN form as well.
