## Facts

### What Is a Schedule E (Form 1040) Used For?

Schedule E is used to **report supplemental income or loss **from rental real estate, royalties, partnerships, S corporations, estates, trusts, and certain REMIC interests. It separates income and losses and **calculates totals that flow through to an individual tax return.**

### Who Should Use a Schedule E (Form 1040)?

You should use Schedule E if you **receive income from rental properties, royalties, or pass-through entities**. It also applies if you are a **beneficiary of an estate or trust or have ownership interests** that generate reportable income or losses.

### When Is the Schedule E (Form 1040) Due?

The Schedule E (Form 1040) is **due by the standard tax deadline, usually April 15. **Extensions apply to the entire return, but taxes owed must still be paid by the original due date to avoid penalties.

### What Happens If a Schedule E (Form 1040) Is Not Submitted?

If a Schedule E (Form 1040) is not submitted, **your tax return may be incomplete or inaccurate. **This can lead to IRS notices, delays, penalties, or interest if income is underreported or losses are incorrectly claimed.

### How and Where Do I File a Schedule E (Form 1040)?

You file Schedule E as part of your Form 1040 **electronically or by mailing it to the IRS. **You may also need to attach additional forms, such as those for depreciation, passive activity limits, or at-risk rules.
