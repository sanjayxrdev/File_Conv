What Is Form 8821 Used For?

Form 8821 is used for authorizing the IRS to disclose your confidential tax information to a person, firm, or organization you name. It covers inspection and receipt of tax records for the years and tax matters you list, but does not permit representation before the IRS.

Who Should Use Form 8821?

Anyone who wants a CPA, bookkeeper, attorney, or other designee to view their IRS records should use Form 8821. Businesses, individuals, partnerships, and corporations can all file it, but if you need someone to represent you before the IRS, use Form 2848 instead.

When Is Form 8821 Due?

Form 8821 has no set due date because it is not a periodic tax return. You file it whenever you need to grant, change, or revoke access to your tax information, and the authorization stays active until revoked or withdrawn.

What Happens If Form 8821 Is Not Submitted?

If Form 8821 is not submitted, your designee cannot inspect or receive your confidential IRS tax information. That lack of access can delay audit preparation, slow down a loan or verification request, or make it harder to meet other tax-related deadlines, but there are generally no Form 8821 penalties for simply not submitting the form.

How and Where Do I File Form 8821?

You can file Form 8821 online through the IRS Submit Forms 2848 and 8821 Online tool, or by fax or mail to the IRS address or fax number listed in the Form 8821 instructions. In some cases, tax professionals can also use IRS Tax Pro Account to request tax information authorization without uploading the form.

Faxed or mailed Forms 8821 require a handwritten signature, while electronically signed forms must be submitted online.

#2. Can I submit Form 8821 electronically?

Yes, you can submit Form 8821 electronically through the IRS Tax Pro Account or the Submit Forms 2848 and 8821 Online tool, both of which confirm receipt right away. Electronic signatures are accepted for this method, though faxed or mailed copies of Form 8821 still require a handwritten signature from the taxpayer.

#3. What if my Form 8821 is missing information?

If your Form 8821 is missing information, it can delay or prevent the IRS from honoring your authorization request entirely and leave your designee without access when you need it most. Missing taxpayer details, designee information, or a signature are common reasons for rejection, so double-check every field against the Form 8821 instructions (and a full Form 8821 example) before you submit the document.

#7. How long does it take to process Form 8821?

How long it takes to process Form 8821 depends on how the authorization is submitted. Certain individual tax information authorization requests made through IRS Tax Pro Account can be processed in real time, with most approved authorizations appearing immediately and some taking up to 48 hours. Forms submitted through the IRS online upload tool, by fax, or by mail may take longer because they are processed separately and can be delayed.

#8. What's the difference between Form 8821 and Form 2848?

Form 8821 only authorizes a designee to inspect or receive your confidential tax information from the IRS, nothing more. Meanwhile, Form 2848 grants power of attorney instead and lets a representative speak for you, sign documents, negotiate directly with the IRS, and otherwise fully represent you in tax matters and proceedings on your behalf.
