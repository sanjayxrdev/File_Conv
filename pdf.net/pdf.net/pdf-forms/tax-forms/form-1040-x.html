What Is a Form 1040-X Used For?

A Form 1040-X is used to amend a previously filed federal tax return. It allows you to correct income, deductions, credits, or filing status, update IRS-adjusted amounts, or claim refunds related to losses or unused credits carried back to prior years.

Who Should Use a Form 1040-X?

Taxpayers who need to fix errors on Form 1040, 1040-SR, or 1040-NR should use Form 1040-X. This includes individuals who received new tax documents, identified reporting mistakes, or needed to update information after the IRS made adjustments.

When Is the Form 1040-X Due?

Form 1040-X is due within three years of filing your original return or within two years of paying the tax, whichever is later. Special rules may extend deadlines in certain situations, such as disasters, foreign tax credits, or bad debts.

What Happens If the Form 1040-X Is Not Submitted?

If a Form 1040-X is not submitted, you may overpay or underpay your taxes. This can result in missed refunds or IRS penalties and interest if discrepancies are later identified during processing or review.

How and Where Do I File a Form 1040-X?

You can file Form 1040-X electronically for the current or two prior tax periods; otherwise, you must print and mail it to the IRS. File a separate form for each tax year and include all required supporting documents.
