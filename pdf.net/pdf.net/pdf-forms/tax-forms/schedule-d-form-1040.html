What Is a Schedule D (Form 1040) Used For?

Schedule D is used to calculate your overall capital gain or loss by combining totals from Form 8949 and other sources. It also reports capital gain distributions, carryover losses, and gains from specific IRS forms tied to investment and asset transactions.

Who Should Use a Schedule D (Form 1040)?

You should use Schedule D if you sold capital assets, received capital gain distributions, or need to report gains or losses from partnerships, trusts, or other forms. It is also required if you are carrying forward prior-year capital losses.

When Is the Schedule D (Form 1040) Due?

Schedule D is due by the standard tax deadline, which is usually April 15, along with your Form 1040. While you can request an extension to file, any taxes owed on capital gains must still be paid by the original due date.

What Happens If a Schedule D (Form 1040) Is Not Submitted?

If your Schedule D (Form 1040) is not submitted, your return may be incomplete. This can trigger IRS notices, penalties, or adjustments, especially if capital gains, losses, or distributions were reported elsewhere but not properly summarized.

How and Where Do I File a Schedule D (Form 1040)?

You file Schedule D with the IRS along with your Form 1040, electronically or by mail. You may also need to attach other supporting forms depending on your transactions.
