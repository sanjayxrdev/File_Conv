What Is Form 2210 Used For?

Form 2210 is used to determine whether an individual, estate, or trust owes a penalty for underpaying the estimated tax to the IRS and figure out the amount if they do. Most taxpayers don’t have to send this form, as the IRS calculates the penalty for them, but there are certain exceptions when it is required.

Also, the IRS automatically waives the penalty in certain situations. For example, if the total tax shown on the return is less than $1,000, or if the taxpayer had no tax liability in the prior year.

Who Should Use Form 2210?

Individuals, estates, and trusts should use Form 2210 in the following situations:

  • You’re requesting a waiver of all or part of your penalty.
  • Your income varied throughout the year and your penalty is reduced or eliminated when figured using the annualized income installment method.
  • You’re electing to treat withholding as paid on the actual date it was withheld, rather than in four equal installments.

For more information, check the flowchart on IRS Form 2210. It will show you when you shouldn’t file this form, when you can but aren’t required to, and when you must.

When Is Form 2210 Due?

Form 2210 is submitted along with a federal income tax return and is due on the same date; generally, April 15th for individuals filing Form 1040. If this date falls on a weekend or a federal holiday, the deadline is pushed to the next business day.

What Happens If Form 2210 Is Not Submitted?

If Form 2210 isn’t submitted, the IRS will calculate your penalty for you and send you a notice. This is fine in most cases, but keep in mind that no waiver or penalty reduction will be taken into account without this form.

How and Where Do I File Form 2210?

File Form 2210 by attaching it to your applicable federal income tax return—Form 1040 for individuals or Form 1041 for estates and trusts—and submitting it as part of the package. E-filing is also available; simply follow the instructions for the federal income tax return to file it correctly.

#1. How do I get a blank Form 2210?

You can get a blank Form 2210 from pdf.net or the official IRS website. Whichever you choose, open it in our editor to quickly fill it out before submission.

#2. Can I submit Form 2210 electronically?

Yes, you can submit Form 2210 electronically, as it is part of the annual federal income tax package. Typically, this is done using the IRS’s Free File platform.

#3. What if my Form 2210 is missing information?

If your Form 2210 is missing information, the IRS will calculate the penalty for you, and you can generally leave it at that. However, If you want to claim a waiver or penalty reduction after filing, submit Form 1040-X, the amended individual income tax return, with a completed Form 2210 attached. To avoid these complications, use a Form 2210 example for guidance when filling out your own.

#6. Can I correct mistakes on Form 2210?

Yes, you can correct mistakes on Form 2210 by filing Form 1040-X and attaching an updated document to it.

#7. How to avoid the Form 2210 penalty?

To avoid the Form 2210 underpayment penalty, you should pay at least 90% of your current year’s tax liability or 100% of the previous year’s, whichever is smaller. You can also request a waiver for specific circumstances, like natural disasters or deaths of immediate family members. Also, the annualized income installment method can eliminate your penalty in certain situations.

#8. How is the 2210 penalty calculated?

The Form 2210 penalty is calculated based on the amount of the underpayment, the period during which it remained unpaid, and the applicable federal interest rate, which is the federal short-term rate plus 3 percentage points. Follow the worksheet provided on the form to figure out the total amount.
