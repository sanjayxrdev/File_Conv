## Facts

### What Is Form 172 Used For?

Form 172 is used for calculating the amount of a net operating loss (NOL)** **that individuals, estates, and trusts can carry back or carry forward to other tax years. It helps reduce taxable income in those years and is commonly used when deductions exceed income due to business, rental, or federally declared disaster-related losses.

### Who Should Use a Form 172?

All individuals, estates, and trusts that have a net operating loss should use Form 172.** **Also,** **partners and S corporation shareholders may use IRS Form 172 to calculate their individual share of business-related losses. However, partnerships and S corporations do not file this form on the entity level; instead, the NOL is reported on Form 1040 or 1041.

On the other hand, corporations do not use Form 172. They use Form 1139 to claim NOL carrybacks instead.

### When Is Form 172 Due?

Form 172 is due when you file the related tax return or amended return used to claim the net operating loss deduction. It is commonly attached to Form 1040, Form 1041, or Form 1040-X when claiming or amending an NOL deduction, or to Form 1045 when applying for a tentative refund.

### What Happens If Form 172 Is Not Submitted?

If Form 172 is not submitted on time, the IRS may delay processing your return, deny or reduce your net operating loss deduction, or request additional information. Missing the Form 172 due date can also delay refunds or affect future carry-forward claims.

### How and Where Do I File Form 172?

Form 172 is attached to your applicable federal tax return and submitted to the IRS along with it. File electronically through IRS-approved tax software where supported, though mailing requirements may vary depending on the attached form.
