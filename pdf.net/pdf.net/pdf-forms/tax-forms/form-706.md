## Facts

### What Is Form 706 Used For?

Form 706 is used for reporting federal estate tax under Chapter 11 of the Internal Revenue Code. Also,** **it is used to calculate generation-skipping transfer (GST) tax on direct skips and to elect portability of the deceased spousal unused exclusion (DSUE) amount.

### Who Should Use a Form 706?

An executor should use a Form 706 for the estate of a deceased U.S. citizen or resident if the total gross estate, adjusted taxable gifts, and specific exemption exceed the federal exemption threshold. Also, IRS Form 706 should be used to elect portability and transfer the DSUE amount to a surviving spouse, even if the estate is below the filing threshold.

Executors of nonresident alien estates should use Form 706-NA instead, as different filing thresholds and rules apply.

### When Is Form 706 Due?

Form 706 is due** **within 9 months after the decedent’s date of death. Executors can request an automatic 6-month extension using Form 4768. For estates not otherwise required to file, Rev. Proc. 2022-32 allows a late portability election up to five years after the decedent's date of death.

### What Happens If Form 706 Is Not Submitted?

If Form 706 is not submitted on time, the estate may face penalties and interest for late filing or late payment. Missing the deadline can prevent an executor from making a timely portability election. Additionally, if no return is filed, the IRS may make its own assessment of the estate tax owed.

### How and Where Do I File a Form 706?

You file Form 706 by mailing it to the IRS. Note that several states impose their own estate taxes with thresholds lower than the federal exemption. IRS Form 706 covers federal obligations only. Check your state's requirements separately.
