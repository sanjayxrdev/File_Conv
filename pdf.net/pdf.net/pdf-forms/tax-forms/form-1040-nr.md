## Facts

### What Is Form 1040-NR Used For?

Form 1040-NR is used by nonresident individuals to report income from U.S. sources for tax purposes. You must file it as long as you are engaged in a business or trade in the U.S., even if you have no income for a particular tax year.

### Who Should Use Form 1040-NR?

All individuals who are engaged in a business or trade in the U.S. but don’t hold a green card or pass the substantial presence test should use Form 1040-NR. You’ll find more information about the substantial presence test on the official IRS website, but the general rule is that you are considered a U.S. resident for tax purposes if you were in the country for 31 days in the previous year.

### When Is the Form 1040-NR Due?

Form 1040-NR is due by April 15th or June 15th if you didn’t receive any wages as an employee subject to U.S. income tax withholding. If you’re filing on behalf of an estate or trust, the deadline is on the 15th day of the 4th month after the tax year ends. All of these dates are moved to the next business day if they fall on a weekend or a national holiday.

### What Happens If Form 1040-NR Is Not Submitted?

If Form 1040-NR isn’t submitted, you’ll be subject to failure-to-file and failure-to-pay penalties for each month that you don’t fulfill your obligations. The former is 5% of the tax owed, up to a maximum of 25%, while the latter is 0.5% of the tax owed, up to the same maximum. On top of this, you’ll accrue interest on your taxes each month, further increasing your tax liability.

### How and Where Do I File Form 1040-NR?

You should file Form 1040-NR with the IRS, either electronically or by mail. The former is faster and more convenient, but you’ll need an account on the official IRS website to be eligible.
