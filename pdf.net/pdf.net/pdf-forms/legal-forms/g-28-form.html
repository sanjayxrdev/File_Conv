What Is Form G-28?

Form G-28 is used to establish eligibility of an attorney or an accredited representative to represent a client in an immigration matter. This document is submitted for each new case, even if eligibility was proven before the Department of Homeland Security in a previous one.

Who Is Required to File Form G-28?

The representative in question is required to file Form G-28, whether they are an attorney, a foreign lawyer who’s allowed to practice in the U.S., or a different accredited individual. Law students and graduates under the direct supervision of an attorney or a representative should also fill out parts of the form, as indicated in the Form G-28 instructions.

When Is Form G-28 Due?

There is no specific due date for Form G-28; it is submitted when an attorney or an accredited representative needs to establish eligibility for their client’s case.

What Are the Penalties for Not Submitting Form G-28?

There are no particular penalties for not submitting Form G-28, but you won’t be recognized as an eligible representative if you don’t file it. This may result in communication issues, case delays, and even application denials if critical deadlines and notices are missed.

Where Do I File Form G-28?

You should file Form G-28 with the institution handling a particular case; so it could be with the U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS), the relevant ICE office, or other agencies. Attach the form to the petition or application when submitting it.

#1. How do I get a blank Form G-28?

You can get a blank G-28 Form from pdf.net or the official USCIS website. Then, you can open it in our editor and complete it there, or, if you’re filing with the USCIS, fill it out using their platform.

#2. How many boxes are in Form G-28?

The boxes in Form G-28 aren’t numbered from start to finish; instead, counting starts from the beginning in each section. It’s more important to know that there are up to six parts to complete.

#3. Can I submit Form G-28 electronically instead of on paper?

You can submit Form G-28 electronically instead of on paper in most cases, but the specifics depend on the institution you’re filing with. Both the USCIS and ICE have dedicated platforms for e-filing that make the process quite easy and quick.

#4. What if my Form G-28 is missing information?

If your G-28 Form is missing information, it likely won’t be accepted, so you won’t be able to represent your client in their immigration cases. This might cause miscommunication, delays, and case rejections, so it’s important to always cross-check your document with a Form G-28 example. That way, you’ll ensure you’ve included everything.

#5. Can I fill out my Form G-28 online?

You can fill out your Form G-28 online by opening it in our PDF editor and completing it there. Alternatively, if you’re filing with the USCIS or ICE, you can use their platforms to fill out the document directly.

#6. Is this form free?

This form is free to view and edit, but not to download. For that, you’ll need to purchase one of pdf.net’s subscription plans.

#7. Can I correct mistakes on Form G-28?

You can correct mistakes on Form G-28, but it’s best to contact the USCIS or another institution you’re filing with and explain your issue. If the error is minor, you’ll likely be allowed to correct it on the document you’ve submitted; otherwise, you might have to file a new form entirely.

#8. What if I forgot to file Form G-28?

If you forgot to file Form G-28, you and your client may experience complications, but luckily, this issue is easy to fix. Send the completed document as soon as you notice your mistake, and it will count, even if it’s a little late.
