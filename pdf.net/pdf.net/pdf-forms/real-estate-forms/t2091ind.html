How to Fill Out a Form T2091IND

Step 1: Open the form in our PDF editor and read the Form T2091IND instructions carefully.

Step 2: Enter the information about the property you’re designating as your principal residence and sign your designation.

Step 3: If you’ve ticked box 2 or 3 at line 17900 on your Schedule 3, complete Section 2 to calculate your capital gain.

Step 4: Attach Form T2091IND to your Schedule 3 and T1.

Step 5: Submit the package to the CRA.

What Is Form T2091IND Used For?

Form T2091IND is used to designate a property as your principal residence, so that you can claim exemption from capital gains tax when selling it. This form is also used to calculate your capital gains in case you designate a property as your principal residence for only some of the years you owned it.

Who Should Use Form T2091IND?

Individuals who have sold their home, or are considered to have done so, should use Form T2091IND if they wish to designate it as their principal residence.

When Is the Form T2091IND Due?

Form T2091IND is due by the same deadline as your personal income tax return: April 30th of the year following the sale. If you are self-employed, the due date is moved to June 15th; in case either of these dates falls on a weekend or a national holiday, the deadline is pushed to the next business day.

Are There Penalties for Not Filing Form T2091IND?

There are significant penalties for not filing Form T2091IND, even if you don’t owe any taxes on capital gains. The fine is the lesser of $8,000 or $100 for each complete month that your return is late.

How and Where Do I File Form T2091IND?

You should file Form T2091IND with the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) at the same time you submit your income tax return (T2) and Schedule 3, used to calculate your capital gains and losses. All these forms should be sent as a package, either by mail or electronically, using certified software.

#1. How do I get a blank Form T2091IND?

You can get a blank Form T2091IND from pdf.net or from the official CRA website. In both cases, it’s easy to fill them out by opening them in our PDF editor and typing information directly into the designated fields.

#2. Can I submit a Form T2091IND electronically?

You can submit Form T2091IND electronically through certified software that you can use to file your T1 return. There are two commonly used options: EFILE and NETFILE.

#3. What if my Form T2091IND is missing information?

If your Form T2091IND is missing information, you may miss out on a capital gains tax exemption or face penalties, so it’s important to correct it right away. To do that, use Form T1-ADJ, the Adjustment Request, and submit your revised T2091IND along with it. You can also go to your account on the CRA website and select the “Change my return” option.

However, it’s best to avoid this extra hassle and make sure your document is correct by comparing it to a Form T2091IND example before submission.

#4. Can I fill out my Form T2091IND online?

You can fill out your Form T2091IND online using our PDF editor that allows you to type the required details directly into the empty slots. Similar options exist on the official CRA website, too.

#5. Is this form free?

This form is free to view and edit, but you can’t download it without a subscription plan. You can find the available options on our pricing page.

#6. Can I correct mistakes on Form T2091IND?

You can correct mistakes on Form T2091IND by submitting T1-ADJ and attaching the revised document to it. It’s advisable to also include a letter explaining what needs to be changed.

#7. Is Form T2091IND required for gifts or inheritances?

Form T2091IND isn’t required for receiving gifts and inheritances, but you must fill it out if you want to designate gifted property as your principal residence and sell it. In this case, the same rules apply for gifted and bought real estate.

#8. Do both buyer and seller need to sign Form T2091IND?

No, only the seller needs to sign Form T2091IND, as they are the one designating property and claiming capital gains tax exemptions. The buyer isn’t involved in this part of the process at all.

#9. Is this form the same as a Real Estate Transfer Tax Declaration?

No, this form isn’t the same as the Real Estate Transfer Tax Declaration, as that document is used to transfer the title of ownership to a new owner and pay a land transfer tax, not designate a property as your principal residence.
