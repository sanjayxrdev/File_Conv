## Facts

### What Is SF-181 Used For?

SF-181 is used for collecting information on federal employees’ and job applicants’ ethnicity and race, which helps the Office of Personnel Management track workforce diversity. This, in turn, is used to support equal employment opportunity.

### Who Should Use SF-181?

Federal employees and applicants for federal jobs are usually asked to fill out SF-181 and provide data about their ethnicity and race. This is completely voluntary, though; you can refuse participation, but the employing agency might try to identify your race by visual observation in that case.

### When Is the SF-181 Due?

SF-181 is usually due during the onboarding process or in the first few days of employment. Aside from that, there is no strict deadline, and employees can update the information on the form whenever they want.

### What Happens If SF-181 Is Not Submitted?

Nothing bad happens if SF-181 is not submitted, because the filing is voluntary. Failing to send in this form won’t affect your employment in any way, nor will you face any penalties.

### How and Where Do I File SF-181?

You should file SF-181 with the HR department of the federal agency you’re applying to or working for. This can be done in several ways: in person, by mail, via email, or through secure online portals when those are available.
