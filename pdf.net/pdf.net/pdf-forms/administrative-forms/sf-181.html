How to Fill Out an SF-181

Step 1: Open SF-181 in our PDF editor.

Step 2: Provide your name, social security number, and birthdate.

Step 3: Read the privacy statement and SF-181 instructions to learn your rights.

Step 4: Answer the first question about being Hispanic or Latino.

Step 5: Read the definitions of each racial category and select the one you identify with.

Step 6: Submit the form to HR.

What Is SF-181 Used For?

SF-181 is used for collecting information on federal employees’ and job applicants’ ethnicity and race, which helps the Office of Personnel Management track workforce diversity. This, in turn, is used to support equal employment opportunity.

Who Should Use SF-181?

Federal employees and applicants for federal jobs are usually asked to fill out SF-181 and provide data about their ethnicity and race. This is completely voluntary, though; you can refuse participation, but the employing agency might try to identify your race by visual observation in that case.

When Is the SF-181 Due?

SF-181 is usually due during the onboarding process or in the first few days of employment. Aside from that, there is no strict deadline, and employees can update the information on the form whenever they want.

What Happens If SF-181 Is Not Submitted?

Nothing bad happens if SF-181 is not submitted, because the filing is voluntary. Failing to send in this form won’t affect your employment in any way, nor will you face any penalties.

How and Where Do I File SF-181?

You should file SF-181 with the HR department of the federal agency you’re applying to or working for. This can be done in several ways: in person, by mail, via email, or through secure online portals when those are available.

#1. How do I get a blank SF-181?

You can get a blank SF-181 from pdf.net or the official OPM website. Your federal employer might also provide you with a copy during the onboarding process, instead of asking you to look for one by yourself.

#2. Can I submit an SF-181 electronically?

You can submit SF-181 electronically by email or using your agency’s secure online portal if such an option exists. This method is preferred by most employees, as it’s easier to keep electronic records than paper ones.

#3. What if my SF-181 is missing information?

If your SF-181 is missing information, your employing agency will likely try to identify your race by visual observation. Aside from that, there are no consequences, and you can submit an updated SF-181 whenever you want. Use an SF-181 example for guidance when doing so.

#4. Can I fill out my SF-181 online?

You can fill out your SF-181 online by using our PDF editor and typing information directly into the empty slots.

#5. Is this form free?

This form is free to view and edit, but you can’t download it unless you have a subscription plan. Check out the available options on our pricing page.

#6. Can I correct mistakes on SF-181?

You can correct mistakes on SF-181 by simply notifying HR and submitting a new form or changing information on the original one. If you’re using an online portal for filling out this document, you may be able to edit and save the information directly on it.

#7. How long does it take to process SF-181?

SF-181 is processed right away and kept on your record. Generally, it doesn’t affect your employment in any way, so you don’t have to worry about the processing time.

#8. Can I submit SF-181 by mail?

You can submit SF-181 by mail if that’s a common practice in your federal agency. You’ll be informed about the available delivery methods during the onboarding, so choose the one that works best for you.

#9. Who can help me if I have questions about SF-181?

If you have questions about SF-181, talk to your agency’s HR department, which can also assist you in filling out the form. However, if you’re more interested in the document’s purpose and usage, contact the OPM or read the information on its website.

#10. How long is an SF-181 valid?

There is no specific validity date for SF-181, as the form is used to collect long-term information that doesn’t typically change. However, if you want to update it at any point, you can do so by contacting HR.
